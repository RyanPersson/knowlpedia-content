## Findings on statements and their sources

1. **Cutoff normalization alone does not define a quotient integral.** The row correctly defines a cutoff. Its next clause needs an s-finite invariant measure μ on the space, an s-finite inversion-invariant measure ν on the group and a measurable invariant integrand. The weight on the space is β dμ. For a counterexample, let a group of order two swap two points, give the group counting measure and put all of μ at the first point. Each singleton indicator satisfies the cutoff equation everywhere, but their weighted integrals of the invariant constant 1 are 1 and 0. [Cutoff row](https://github.com/RyanPersson/knowlpedia/blob/12cc7330341b6b4fb72c78b91a659e67e82a4b18/docs/riemann2/concepts/spanning-unfolding-b.tsv#L27) · [Lean cutoff-independence theorem](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean/blob/72fead102f663d5c7a407964a13dc0cf9d977207/grh/src/Lib/Adelic/QuotientMeasure/Basic.lean#L215)

2. **The normalized sigma correction leaves two misleading Lean names.** The classical sigma row points to `New.CMLattice.σ`. The declaration is `PeriodPair.σ` and defines the normalized theta-space element, which belongs on row 44 only. Remove its association with the classical product. The generic theta row should cite `New.RealComplexForm.thetaSpace`. `New.ThetaTori.LatticeForm` is the type tag of its argument. Also name the translated multiplier in the CM specialization. Row 44 uses χ for the parity sign of sigma, whereas sections of degree m use `(C.multiplier)^m`, including the Riemann phase of the translation by t. 3. **The linked Hilbert–Schmidt knowl uses the opposite inner-product convention.** Keep the target and identify the convention beside the polarization formula. Lean gives ⟨A,B⟩ = tr(A*B), linear in B. The target gives tr(B*A), linear in A. For the one-dimensional maps A = 1 and B = i the two values are i and −i. Both give the stated squared norm on the diagonal. 4. **Two formulas need domain conditions.** The last-column form of Cramer’s rule needs a square matrix of positive size. An empty matrix has no last column. The finite-family Kronecker product uses a commutative scalar ring. For a two-element index set, scalar 1×1 matrices with noncommuting entries would give different answers in the two orders. State these conditions in the respective rows. 6. **The Bochner and positive-definite rows need a condition or an attribution.** Bochner integrability requires almost-everywhere strong measurability and a finite integral of the norm. A constant 1 on ℝ with Lebesgue measure is strongly measurable but fails that criterion. For a complex positive-definite function the absolute-value estimate stated in the sidecar follows from Cauchy–Schwarz. The specific Lean lemma quoted proves the one-sided inequality β(s) ≤ β(1) for a real-valued β. Name both statements when retaining the broader bound.
