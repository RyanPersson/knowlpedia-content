## Comments on the translation

1. **Grid centres, proposition 3.** The proposition identifies the *doubled* node parameter with $\lambda_B$, which the centre-system knowl defines in half-integer units. Take $z=1$ and $o=i=j=0$. There are no blocks or lowering. The displayed stem entry is $\lambda^Y_{\tau,n}+1/2$, while the node entry is $2\lambda^Y_{\tau,n}+1$. State that the doubled node vector lists the entries of $2\lambda_B$ with the specified signs. The Lean definition of `IsParam` explicitly uses this convention. [Grid proposition](https://github.com/RyanPersson/knowlpedia-content/blob/1e9a486456f7a631e9492ceb29b305431faea78d/content/proofs/grh/upper-objects/grid-centres.knowl.md#L28) · [Definition of $\lambda_B$](https://github.com/RyanPersson/knowlpedia-content/blob/1e9a486456f7a631e9492ceb29b305431faea78d/content/proofs/grh/upper-objects/centre-system.knowl.md#L36) · [Node formula](https://github.com/RyanPersson/knowlpedia-content/blob/1e9a486456f7a631e9492ceb29b305431faea78d/content/proofs/grh/upper-objects/grid-nodes.knowl.md#L20) · [Lean convention](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean/blob/72fead102f663d5c7a407964a13dc0cf9d977207/grh/src/New/UpperBound/Objects/InsertBlock.lean#L94)

   The same proposition also admits $o=j=0$ and $i=m+1$. Then $B$ contains $u_m$, although the knowl defines centres only for $l<m$ and defines $\lambda_B$ only for $B\subseteq C$. Adding $i\le m$ gives a valid statement for the finite grid. To retain the full Lean statement, give values, lengths, and kinds on the ambient label set $\mathbb N\sqcup\mathbb N$ and allow any finite $B$ there. The distinguished finite set $C$ still determines separation and which missing centres contribute signs. [Grid labels](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean/blob/72fead102f663d5c7a407964a13dc0cf9d977207/grh/src/New/UpperBound/Objects/Parameters.lean#L504) · [Subset lemma](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean/blob/72fead102f663d5c7a407964a13dc0cf9d977207/grh/src/New/UpperBound/Objects/Parameters.lean#L538) · [Parameter theorem](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean/blob/72fead102f663d5c7a407964a13dc0cf9d977207/grh/src/New/UpperBound/Objects/Parameters.lean#L835)

2. **Height bound for a constant family.** The final remark suggests a comparison with the logarithmic Weil height. For $y_\iota=2$, take $H=1/2$, $\alpha=-\log 2$, and every $h_\ell=0$. Every conjugate of $H$ has absolute value $1/2$, and $Hy_\iota=1$ at every finite place. Thus `HeightLe` holds with $c=-\log 2$, whereas the usual logarithmic Weil height of $2$ is $\log 2$, already in degree one. Replace the comparison with this example to illustrate what a negative bound means. [Height remark](https://github.com/RyanPersson/knowlpedia-content/blob/1e9a486456f7a631e9492ceb29b305431faea78d/content/proofs/grh/reduction-main/height-bound.knowl.md#L31) · [Lean definition](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean/blob/72fead102f663d5c7a407964a13dc0cf9d977207/grh/src/New/Reduction/Lattices/Heights.lean#L41)

3. **Gaussian theta series.** The definition introduces an ordinary infinite sum for arbitrary coefficients and real $u$. Even with $u=1$ and $j=0$, taking $c(n)=e^{\pi n^2}$ makes every summand equal to $1$. With bounded coefficients $c(n)=1$, the same divergence occurs at $u=0$ for $j=0$. Introduce the classical series under the assumptions already used in Properties, $u>0$ and $|c(n)|\le1$, or state explicitly how Lean assigns a value to a nonsummable series. [Translation](https://github.com/RyanPersson/knowlpedia-content/blob/1e9a486456f7a631e9492ceb29b305431faea78d/content/proofs/grh/reduction-zeros/theta-series-of-coefficients.knowl.md#L14) · [Lean definition](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean/blob/72fead102f663d5c7a407964a13dc0cf9d977207/grh/src/Lib/Analytic/DirichletTheta.lean#L31) · [Summability lemma](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean/blob/72fead102f663d5c7a407964a13dc0cf9d977207/grh/src/Lib/Analytic/DirichletTheta.lean#L110)

4. **Distinguished factor in the six-copy valuation proof.** The preparation sets $\delta_1=1$, then step 1 singles out factor $0$ in $G(z',z'')=F(z'+z'')\prod_{c\ne0}F(\delta_c z')$. To identify $G(z',0)$ with the integral product $U$, write $\delta_0,\ldots,\delta_5$ with $\delta_0=1$. This agrees with both the cited coefficient lemma and the six factors in Lean. [Valuation proof](https://github.com/RyanPersson/knowlpedia-content/blob/1e9a486456f7a631e9492ceb29b305431faea78d/content/proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/least-valuation-along-couplings.knowl.md#L27) · [Coefficient lemma in the translation](https://github.com/RyanPersson/knowlpedia-content/blob/1e9a486456f7a631e9492ceb29b305431faea78d/content/proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/coefficient-of-a-product-of-couplings.knowl.md#L17) · [Lean six-copy lemma](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean/blob/72fead102f663d5c7a407964a13dc0cf9d977207/grh/src/New/Derivatives/Sharpness/Couplings.lean#L471)

5. **Real argument in the one-sided criterion.** The final remark links the application to `eventually_primeRemainder_natCast_le`, the estimate at integer arguments. Point it to `eventually_primeRemainder_le_rpow` or to step 2 of the translated one-sided bound. For $y\ge2$, Lean bounds $R(y)-R(\lfloor y\rfloor)$ by $26/y$, bounds $\lfloor y\rfloor^t$ by $2y^t$ for $-1\le t\le0$, and absorbs $2C+26$ into $y^\delta$. This gives the estimate at every sufficiently large **real** argument required by the criterion. [Criterion remark](https://github.com/RyanPersson/knowlpedia-content/blob/1e9a486456f7a631e9492ceb29b305431faea78d/content/proofs/grh/reduction-zeros/one-sided-criterion.knowl.md#L53) · [Translated argument](https://github.com/RyanPersson/knowlpedia-content/blob/1e9a486456f7a631e9492ceb29b305431faea78d/content/proofs/grh/reduction-main/one-sided-bound.knowl.md#L37) · [Lean calculation](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean/blob/72fead102f663d5c7a407964a13dc0cf9d977207/grh/src/New/Reduction/OneSidedBound.lean#L219) · [Application in `New.grh`](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean/blob/72fead102f663d5c7a407964a13dc0cf9d977207/grh/src/New/Reduction/OneSidedBound.lean#L255)

6. **A declaration has two link targets.** Both the half-plane definition and the final theorem claim `DirichletLNonvanishingRightOfHalf` in their `lean` metadata. The resolver keeps the first page it encounters for a declaration. Keep this name on the definition, keep `New.grh` on the theorem, and link the theorem to the definition. This gives each Lean name one destination.
