For a finite alphabet \(A\) with [[probability/probability-measure|probability distribution]] \(p\), the **two-sided Bernoulli shift** is
\[
X=A^{\mathbb Z},\qquad \mu=p^{\mathbb Z},\qquad (Tx)_j=x_{j+1}.
\]
The product sigma-algebra is generated by events depending on finitely many coordinates. The shift is invertible and measure preserving, and is strongly mixing.

## Why it mixes

Two events depending on finite coordinate sets become independent when a sufficiently large shift separates those sets. Hence their correlation then equals the product of their probabilities exactly. Approximation by these finite-coordinate events proves mixing for all measurable events.

## One-sided version

On \(A^{\mathbb N_0}\), the same formula drops the initial coordinate. Unless \(p\) is concentrated at one symbol, this version is noninvertible; its forward Koopman operator remains an isometry. Binary digits identify the fair one-sided binary shift with the doubling map modulo the usual null set of ambiguous expansions.
