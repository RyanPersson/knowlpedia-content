A probability-preserving transformation \(T\) is **totally ergodic** if \(T^m\) is [[ergodic-theory/ergodic-transformation|ergodic]] for every integer \(m\geq1\).

## Spectral criterion

For an invertible ergodic system, this is equivalent to the absence of Koopman eigenvalues that are nontrivial roots of unity. Indeed, such an eigenfunction becomes fixed under a power. Conversely, a nonconstant vector fixed by \(U^m\) lies in a finite-dimensional invariant space generated by its first \(m\) iterates, which has a root-of-unity eigenvector.

Irrational torus translations are totally ergodic. A single finite cycle of length greater than one is ergodic but not totally ergodic. Weak mixing implies total ergodicity, but irrational rotations show that the converse fails.
