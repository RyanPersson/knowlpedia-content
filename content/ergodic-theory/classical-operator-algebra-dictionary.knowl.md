An invertible [[ergodic-theory/measure-preserving-system|probability-preserving system]] \((X,\Sigma,\mu,T)\) determines
\[
A=L^\infty(X,\mu),\qquad\varphi(f)=\int_X f\,d\mu,
\qquad\alpha(f)=f\circ T^{-1}.
\]
Here \(A\) is a commutative von Neumann algebra acting by multiplication on \(L^2(X,\mu)\), \(\varphi\) is a [[operator-algebras/faithful-normal-state|faithful normal state]], and \(\alpha\) is a normal [[operator-algebras/state-preserving-dynamical-system|state-preserving]] \(*\)-automorphism.

## Events and algebra

Pointwise multiplication and complex conjugation give \(f^*f=|f|^2\). An event \(E\), modulo null sets, corresponds to a [[operator-algebras/projection-cstar-algebra|projection]] \(1_E=1_E^*=1_E^2\), and \(\varphi(1_E)=\mu(E)\). Every projection of \(A\) arises this way. Ergodicity becomes \(A^\alpha=\mathbb C1\).

## Recovering a measurable model

On standard probability spaces, normal state-preserving isomorphisms of these function algebras correspond to measure-space isomorphisms modulo null sets. This recovers the measured system, not a preferred topology or individual representatives on null sets.

## Koopman covariance

If \(Kf=f\circ T^{-1}\) and \(M_f\) is multiplication by \(f\), then
\[
K M_f K^*=M_{\alpha(f)}.
\]
The [[operator-algebras/group-measure-space-construction|crossed product]] uses a regular representation on a larger Hilbert space; the covariance identity alone does not identify the algebra generated by \(M_f,K\) with that crossed product.
