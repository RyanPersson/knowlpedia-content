A **transcendence basis** of a [[algebra-fields-galois/field-extension|field extension]] \(L/K\) is an [[algebra-fields-galois/algebraic-independence|algebraically independent]] subset \(B\subseteq L\) such that \(L\) is [[algebra-fields-galois/algebraic-extension|algebraic]] over the field \(K(B)\) generated by \(K\) and \(B\).

## Existence

Order the algebraically independent subsets by inclusion. The union of a chain is independent because a polynomial relation involves only finitely many elements, which would already lie together in one member of the chain. [[shared-foundations/zorns-lemma|Zorn's lemma]] gives a maximal independent subset \(B\). If \(x\) were transcendental over \(K(B)\), any polynomial relation on \(B\cup\{x\}\) could be collected in powers of \(x\). Independence of \(B\) makes at least one coefficient nonzero, contradicting transcendence of \(x\). Thus \(L\) is algebraic over \(K(B)\).
