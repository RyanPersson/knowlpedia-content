An **irrational number** is a [[shared-foundations/real-numbers|real number]] outside \(\mathbb Q\), the set of [[shared-foundations/rational-numbers|rational numbers]]. Equivalently, it cannot be expressed as \(p/q\) with integers \(p,q\) and \(q\ne0\).

## Example

The positive square root of \(2\) is irrational. If \(\sqrt2=p/q\) in lowest terms with \(q>0\), then \(p^2=2q^2\). The parity of squares implies \(p\) is even, and substitution then implies \(q\) is even, contradicting lowest terms. Irrationality alone does not give a specified quantitative rate at which rational numbers can approximate a number.
