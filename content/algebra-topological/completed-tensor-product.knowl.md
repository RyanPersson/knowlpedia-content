Let \(k\) be a commutative ring, let \(M\) have a decreasing filtration
\(M=M_0\supseteq M_1\supseteq\cdots\), and let \(N\) have a decreasing
filtration \(N=N_0\supseteq N_1\supseteq\cdots\). Their **completed tensor
product** for these filtrations is
\[
M\mathbin{\widehat\otimes}_k N
:=
\varprojlim_{r,s}
\bigl(M/M_r\bigr)\otimes_k\bigl(N/N_s\bigr).
\]
It receives the ordinary [[algebra-modules/tensor-product|tensor product]]
\(M\otimes_k N\) through the compatible quotient maps. The symbol
\(\widehat\otimes\) is therefore meaningful only after the filtrations or
topologies being completed have been specified.

## Adic algebras

If \(A\) and \(B\) are complete separated \(k\)-algebras with ideals of
definition \(I\subseteq A\) and \(J\subseteq B\), the adic completed tensor
product is
\[
A\mathbin{\widehat\otimes}_k B
=
\varprojlim_{r,s}
(A/I^r)\otimes_k(B/J^s).
\]
Under standard hypotheses this is the completion of \(A\otimes_k B\) for the
ideal generated by \(I\otimes B\) and \(A\otimes J\). For example,
\[
k[[x_1,\ldots,x_m]]
\mathbin{\widehat\otimes}_k
k[[y_1,\ldots,y_n]]
\cong
k[[x_1,\ldots,x_m,y_1,\ldots,y_n]]
\]
with the adic topologies defined by the variables.

## Universal property

For complete separated targets \(P\), continuous bilinear maps
\(M\times N\to P\) that are compatible with the chosen linear topologies
factor through continuous linear maps
\[
M\widehat\otimes_k N\longrightarrow P
\]
when the usual completeness and exactness hypotheses for the chosen category
are satisfied. This is the topological analogue of the tensor-product
universal property.

## Convention warning

There is no single completed tensor product for every kind of topological
module. Functional analysis distinguishes projective, injective, Hilbert, and
other tensor topologies, while adic algebra uses linear topologies arising
from ideals or submodules. This knowl uses the inverse-limit, linearly
topologized convention needed for formal geometry. It should not be silently
identified with a Banach- or \(C^*\)-algebra tensor product.

## Role in formal geometry

If \(X=\operatorname{Spf}A\) and \(Y=\operatorname{Spf}B\) are affine adic
formal schemes over \(k\), their fiber product has coordinate algebra
\(A\widehat\otimes_k B\) in the standard affine setup. Consequently, a
multiplication \(G\times G\to G\) on an affine formal group pulls back to a
continuous comultiplication
\(\mathcal O(G)\to\mathcal O(G)\widehat\otimes_k\mathcal O(G)\).
