For measurable spaces \((X,\mathcal A)\) and \((Y,\mathcal B)\), their **product sigma-algebra** is
\[
\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal B
=\sigma\{A\times B:A\in\mathcal A,\ B\in\mathcal B\}.
\]
Thus it is [[measure-theory/generated-sigma-algebra|generated]] by [[measure-theory/measurable-rectangle|measurable rectangles]]. It is the smallest sigma-algebra making both coordinate projections measurable.

## Sections

For every set in this sigma-algebra, fixing one coordinate gives a measurable section in the other space. To see this, observe that the sets with that property form a sigma-algebra containing all rectangles. Even when the factor measures are complete, their product sigma-algebra need not contain every subset of a product-null set.
