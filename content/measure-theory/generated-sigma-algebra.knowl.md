For a family \(\mathcal C\) of subsets of \(X\), the **generated sigma-algebra** \(\sigma(\mathcal C)\) is the intersection of all [[measure-theory/sigma-algebra|sigma-algebras]] on \(X\) containing \(\mathcal C\). This collection is nonempty because it contains the full power set. An intersection of sigma-algebras is a sigma-algebra, so the definition gives the unique smallest one containing \(\mathcal C\).

## Generators

To prove that a sigma-algebra contains \(\sigma(\mathcal C)\), it suffices to show it contains every member of \(\mathcal C\). For example, the Borel sigma-algebra is generated by open sets. The generating family need not itself be closed under complements or countable unions.
