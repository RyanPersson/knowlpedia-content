For a prime power \(q\), let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional vector space over \(\mathbb F_q\), and let \(Q:V\to\mathbb F_q\) be a nonsingular [[linear-algebra/quadratic-form|quadratic form]]. The **projective orthogonal derived group** is
\[
 \operatorname{P}\Omega(V,Q)=\Omega(V,Q)/(\Omega(V,Q)\cap\{\lambda I:\lambda\in\mathbb F_q^\times\}),
 \qquad \Omega(V,Q)=[\operatorname O(V,Q),\operatorname O(V,Q)].
\]
Here
\[
 \operatorname O(V,Q)=\{g\in\operatorname{GL}(V):Q(gv)=Q(v)\text{ for all }v\},
\]
and brackets denote the [[algebra-groups/commutator-subgroup|commutator subgroup]], generated by \(aba^{-1}b^{-1}\). Nonsingular means \(\{v\in\operatorname{rad}B:Q(v)=0\}=\{0\}\), where \(B(v,w)=Q(v+w)-Q(v)-Q(w)\) and \(\operatorname{rad}B=\{v:B(v,w)=0\text{ for every }w\}\). Multiplication in the projective group is multiplication of scalar cosets.

## Characteristic two

A quadratic form satisfies \(Q(av)=a^2Q(v)\), with bilinear polar form \(B\). In characteristic two, \(B\) does not determine \(Q\). In even dimension, nonsingularity makes \(B\) nondegenerate. In odd dimension, its radical has dimension one and \(Q\) is nonzero on every nonzero radical vector.

Over an even field in odd dimension \(2n+1\), the action on \(V/\operatorname{rad}B\) identifies the full orthogonal group with \(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(q)\). In the stable simple ranges this also identifies its derived group with the symplectic group. At very small parameters the derived subgroup can be proper.

## Signs and low ranks

For dimension \(2n\), the plus sign means Witt index \(n\): one may take \(Q=\sum_{i=1}^n x_i y_i\). The minus sign means Witt index \(n-1\): use \(n-1\) such hyperbolic planes and the anisotropic plane \(Q(z)=N_{\mathbb F_{q^2}/\mathbb F_q}(z)=z^{q+1}\). Witt index is the largest dimension of a subspace on which \(Q\) vanishes identically. In odd dimension use \(Q=\sum_{i=1}^n x_i y_i+z^2\).

Taking determinant one alone is insufficient: the derived subgroup may be smaller, and in characteristic two every orthogonal transformation has determinant one. This catalogue uses the displayed derived-group convention and explicitly excludes exceptional low parameters from low-rank identifications when necessary.
