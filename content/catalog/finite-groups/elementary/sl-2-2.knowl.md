The **finite matrix group \(\mathrm{SL}_2(2)\)** consists of [[linear-algebra/determinant|determinant-one]] \(2\times2\) matrices over the [[catalog/arithmetic/f2|field \(\mathbb F_2\)]], with matrix multiplication.

## Order and nonsimplicity

There are \((2^2-1)(2^2-2)=6\) invertible matrices. Every nonzero determinant equals one, so \(\mathrm{GL}_2(2)=\mathrm{SL}_2(2)\). Acting on the three nonzero vectors gives an injective map to \(S_3\): a matrix fixing all three vectors fixes a basis. Both groups have order six, so this is an isomorphism. The [[algebra-groups/normal-subgroup|normal subgroup]] corresponding to \(A_3\) shows that it is not simple.
