The **\(2\)-adic integer ring** is the closed unit ball
\[
\mathbb Z_2=\{x\in\mathbb Q_2:|x|_2\le1\}
\]
in [[catalog/arithmetic/q2|\(\mathbb Q_2\)]], with inherited addition, multiplication and \(2\)-adic topology. Equivalently it is the inverse limit \(\varprojlim_m\mathbb Z/2^m\mathbb Z\) under reduction maps.

## Digits and residue

Every element has a unique convergent expansion \(\sum_{j\ge0}a_j2^j\), with digits \(a_j\in\{0,\ldots,2-1\}\). The constant digit is reduction modulo the [[algebra-rings/maximal-ideal|maximal ideal]] \(2\mathbb Z_2\); the [[algebra-commutative/residue-field|residue field]] is [[catalog/arithmetic/f2|\(\mathbb F_2\)]].

## Ring rather than field

The unit group consists of elements with nonzero constant digit. The ring is compact, complete and infinite, and \(2\) is not a unit. In particular it is neither the finite ring \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\) nor a rational vector space.
