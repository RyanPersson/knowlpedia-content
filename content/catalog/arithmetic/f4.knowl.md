The **field \(\mathbb F_4\)** is the [[algebra-fields-galois/finite-field|finite field]] presented by
\[
\mathbb F_4=\mathbb F_2[x]/(x^2+x+1).
\]
Writing \(\alpha\) for the residue class of \(x\), every element has a unique representative of degree less than \(2\), and \(\alpha^2=\alpha+1\).

## Why this is a field

The polynomial \(x^2+x+1\) has no root in \(\mathbb F_2\). Since its degree is two, it is irreducible; the quotient is therefore a field. Counting coefficients gives exactly 4 elements.

## Field maps versus linear maps

The [[algebra-fields-galois/field-automorphism|field automorphism]] group has order \(2\), generated by \(a\mapsto a^2\). The additive space has dimension \(2\) over \(\mathbb F_2\), so its linear automorphism group is \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_2)\). A general invertible linear map need not preserve products or the unit.

## Recorded linear maps

Its [[catalog/arithmetic/finite-field-linear-and-field-maps|linear endomorphism ring]] over \(\mathbb F_2\) is \(M_2(\mathbb F_2)\), with 16 maps. Its invertible linear maps form \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_2)\), with 6 elements. The catalogue keeps this scalar category separate from field morphisms, for which only the 2 Frobenius powers occur.
