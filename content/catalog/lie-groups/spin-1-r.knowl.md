\(\operatorname{Spin}(1,\mathbb R)\) is the [[lie-groups/spin-group|spin group]] in the even [[differential-geometry/clifford-algebra|Clifford algebra]] of \(\mathbb R^{1}\) with [[linear-algebra/quadratic-form|quadratic form]] \(Q(v)=\sum_jv_j^2\) and convention \(v^2=-Q(v)1\). It is generated by products of an even number of vectors satisfying \(Q(v)=1\).

## Dimensions and structure

This group has real dimension \(0\). Conjugation on the generating [[linear-algebra/vector-space|vector space]] gives a two-to-one homomorphism to \(\mathrm{SO}(1,\mathbb R)\) with kernel \(\{\pm1\}\). In dimension one this is the discrete group \(\{\pm1\}\), and SO(1) is trivial. It is not connected.

Clifford conjugation gives a two-sheeted covering with kernel {±1}. See [[catalog/lie-groups/so-1-r|\(\operatorname{SO}(1,\mathbb R)\)]].

In the one-dimensional Clifford algebra, even products of [[linear-algebra/unit-vector|unit vectors]] are exactly {±1}; identify each scalar with the same one-by-one orthogonal matrix. See [[catalog/lie-groups/o-1-r|\(\operatorname{O}(1,\mathbb R)\)]].

## One-dimensional calculation

Let \(e\) be the basis vector with \(e^2=-1\). The only vectors satisfying \(Q(v)=1\) are \(e\) and \(-e\), and their even products are exactly \(1,-1\). They commute with \(e\), so both act trivially on the one-dimensional vector space.
