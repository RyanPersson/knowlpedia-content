Let \(J\) be a smooth
[[differential-geometry/almost-complex-structure|almost-complex structure]]
on a smooth manifold \(M\), and let \(N_J\) be its
[[differential-geometry/nijenhuis-tensor|Nijenhuis tensor]]. The
**Newlander–Nirenberg theorem** states
\[
J\text{ is integrable}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
N_J=0.
\]
Equivalently, vanishing of the Nijenhuis tensor guarantees local complex
coordinates whose standard multiplication by \(i\) induces \(J\).

The theorem is local: it produces a
[[differential-geometry/complex-manifold|complex-manifold structure]], not one
global coordinate chart. The statement here is in the \(C^\infty\) category;
finite- and low-regularity versions require their own hypotheses. In real
dimension two, every smooth almost-complex structure satisfies the criterion
and is therefore integrable.

## Reference

August Newlander and Louis Nirenberg, “Complex Analytic Coordinates in Almost
Complex Manifolds,” *Annals of Mathematics* 65 (1957), 391–404.
[DOI record](https://doi.org/10.2307/1970051).
