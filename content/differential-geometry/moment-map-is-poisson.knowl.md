Let \(G\) act on the [[differential-geometry/symplectic-manifold|symplectic manifold]] \((M,\omega)\), using \(\iota_{X_f}\omega=df\), and let \(\mu:M\to\mathfrak g^*\) be an [[differential-geometry/equivariant-moment-map|equivariant moment map]]. Equip \(\mathfrak g^*\) with the plus Lie–Poisson bracket determined by
\[
\{\ell_\xi,\ell_\eta\}_{\mathfrak g^*}(\alpha)
=\langle\alpha,[\xi,\eta]\rangle,
\qquad \ell_\xi(\alpha)=\langle\alpha,\xi\rangle.
\]
Then \(\mu\) is a [[differential-geometry/poisson-map|Poisson map]]. Conversely, a [[fiber-bundles/moment-map|moment map]] that is Poisson is infinitesimally equivariant; if \(G\) is connected, this infinitesimal condition implies coadjoint equivariance. Equivalently, pullback by \(\mu\) preserves Poisson brackets of smooth functions. The forward implication requires no connectedness hypothesis.

## Proof idea

Write \(\mu^\xi=\ell_\xi\circ\mu\). The moment-map identity says \(X_{\mu^\xi}=\xi_M\). Equivariance differentiates to the bracket identity
\[
\{\mu^\xi,\mu^\eta\}_M=\mu^{[\xi,\eta]}.
\]
This verifies preservation of the Lie–Poisson bracket on linear functions. The Leibniz rule and a local-coordinate argument then extend the equality to arbitrary smooth functions on \(\mathfrak g^*\). Conversely, the Poisson property applied to linear functions gives the displayed infinitesimal identity.

## Coadjoint orbits

The symplectic leaves of the plus Lie–Poisson structure are [[differential-geometry/coadjoint-orbit|coadjoint orbits]] equipped with the compatible [[differential-geometry/kirillov-kostant-souriau-form|Kirillov–Kostant–Souriau form]]. Consequently, an equivariant moment map sends [[differential-geometry/hamiltonian-vector-field|Hamiltonian vector fields]] generated by its components to the corresponding Lie–Poisson Hamiltonian fields and maps each \(G\)-orbit into a coadjoint orbit.

## Conventions and scope

**Warning.** Reversing either the moment-map identity, the Hamiltonian-vector-field convention, or the Lie–Poisson bracket changes a sign in the theorem. Equivariance alone has no sign, but the statement “\(\mu\) is Poisson” is valid only after all three conventions are matched. Connectedness is needed for the converse from infinitesimal to global equivariance, not for the forward implication.
