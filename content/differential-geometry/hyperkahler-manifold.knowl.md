A **hyperkähler manifold** is a [[differential-geometry/riemannian-manifold|Riemannian manifold]] \((M^{4n},g)\) equipped with complex structures \(I,J,K\) satisfying \(IJ=K=-JI\), such that
\[
\nabla I=\nabla J=\nabla K=0
\]
for the [[fiber-bundles/levicivita-connection-connection|Levi-Civita connection]] \(\nabla\) of \(g\). Thus \((M,I,J,K,g)\) is [[differential-geometry/hyperhermitian-manifold|hyper-Hermitian]], and each pair \((g,I)\), \((g,J)\), and \((g,K)\) is Kähler. The ordered triple and metric are part of the structure. Equivalently, one may require the three [[differential-geometry/fundamental-form-almost-hermitian|fundamental forms]] \(\omega_I,\omega_J,\omega_K\) to be closed. No compactness, simple connectedness, or irreducibility hypothesis is included.

## Holonomy and complex-symplectic structure

The parallel triple reduces the [[fiber-bundles/holonomy-group|holonomy group]] to the [[lie-groups/compact-symplectic-group|compact symplectic group \(\operatorname{Sp}(n)\)]]. Conversely, a Riemannian \(4n\)-manifold with holonomy contained in \(\operatorname{Sp}(n)\) locally carries such a parallel triple; a global triple requires the corresponding global reduction. The complex two-form
\[
\Omega_I=\omega_J+i\omega_K
\]
is a parallel [[differential-geometry/holomorphic-symplectic-form|holomorphic symplectic form]] on the [[differential-geometry/complex-manifold|complex manifold]] \((M,I)\). These equivalences are treated in [Joyce, Chapter 7](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780198506010.001.0001).

## Structure and examples

Hyperkähler metrics are Ricci-flat because \(\operatorname{Sp}(n)\subseteq\operatorname{SU}(2n)\). Every unit vector \((a,b,c)\in S^2\) gives another parallel complex structure \(aI+bJ+cK\) and another [[differential-geometry/kahler-form|Kähler form]] \(a\omega_I+b\omega_J+c\omega_K\).

Flat quaternionic space \(\mathbb H^n\) is the basic noncompact example. A Riemannian manifold that is Kähler only for \(I\) is a near miss: the additional parallel anticommuting structures \(J,K\) are essential.

## Terminology

In algebraic geometry, “compact hyperkähler manifold” is sometimes used for an **[[differential-geometry/holomorphic-symplectic-manifold|irreducible holomorphic symplectic manifold]]**: a compact simply connected [[differential-geometry/kahler-manifold|Kähler manifold]] whose holomorphic two-forms are generated by a nondegenerate one. That convention excludes flat complex tori and nontrivial products. The core uses the broader Riemannian convention; the relationship is discussed in [Huybrechts, §1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s002220050280).
