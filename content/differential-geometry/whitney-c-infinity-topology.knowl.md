For [[fiber-bundles/smooth-manifold|smooth manifolds]] \(M\) and \(N\), the **Whitney \(C^\infty\) topology** on \(C^\infty(M,N)\) is the topology generated by simultaneous, locally finite control of the derivatives of maps. Precisely, for each finite \(r\), one gives \(C^\infty(M,N)\) the strong Whitney \(C^r\) topology by requiring the [[differential-geometry/jet-of-a-smooth-map|\(r\)-jet]] \(j^rf:M\to J^r(M,N)\) to lie in a prescribed open subset of the [[fiber-bundles/jet-bundle|jet bundle]], and then takes the topology generated by all \(r\). The allowed tolerances may vary over \(M\), so the definition is stronger than uniform control on a fixed [[topology/compact-set|compact set]].

## Neighborhoods and convergence

Using charts and a locally finite family of compact sets, a basic neighborhood constrains finitely many derivatives on every member of that family, with bounds that may depend on the member. When \(M\) is compact, the strong and weak \(C^\infty\) topologies agree. For noncompact \(M\), convergence uniformly on each compact set generally does not imply strong Whitney convergence.

## Role in differential topology

This topology is the standard setting for stability and genericity results involving global perturbations. Transversality theorems often assert that a specified class of maps is [[topology/residual-set|residual]], dense, or open in an appropriate Whitney topology; the precise choice of strong or weak topology matters on noncompact source manifolds.

## Conventions and scope

**Warning.** Authors do not use “Whitney topology” uniformly. Some reserve it for the strong topology, while others attach the name to compact-open \(C^\infty\) variants. “Fine” may also refer to related refinements. A statement about openness, density, or continuity should therefore name the topology explicitly.
