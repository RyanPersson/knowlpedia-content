A **symplectic manifold** is a pair \((M,\omega)\) where \(M\) is a [[fiber-bundles/smooth-manifold|smooth manifold]] and \(\omega\in\Omega^2(M)\) is a differential \(2\)-form satisfying:

1. **Nondegeneracy:** for every \(p\in M\), the bilinear form \(\omega_p:T_pM\times T_pM\to\mathbb R\) is nondegenerate. Equivalently,
   \[
   \iota_v\omega_p=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad v=0,
   \]
   so \(v\mapsto\iota_v\omega_p\) identifies \(T_pM\) with \(T_p^*M\).

2. **Closedness:** \(d\omega=0\).

Nondegeneracy is pointwise linear algebra, while closedness is a differential condition. A nondegenerate two-form that is not closed defines only an [[differential-geometry/almost-symplectic-manifold|almost-symplectic manifold]].

Closedness also gives the basic integral consequence
\[
\int_{\partial C}\omega=\int_C d\omega=0
\]
for every smooth singular \(3\)-chain \(C\), by
[[differential-geometry/stokes-theorem|Stokes' theorem]].

## Dimension, orientation, and local form

The dimension of \(M\) is even, say \(2n\), and
\[
\frac{\omega^n}{n!}
\]
is a nowhere-vanishing top-degree form. It supplies a canonical orientation and volume form. The [[differential-geometry/darboux-theorem-symplectic|symplectic Darboux theorem]] says that every point has local coordinates \((q_1,\ldots,q_n,p_1,\ldots,p_n)\) in which
\[
\omega=\sum_{i=1}^n dq_i\wedge dp_i.
\]
Thus symplectic forms of a fixed dimension have no local invariants analogous to Riemannian curvature; their distinguishing phenomena are global.

## Maps and dynamics

A [[differential-geometry/symplectic-map|symplectic map]] \(f:(M,\omega_M)\to(N,\omega_N)\) satisfies \(f^*\omega_N=\omega_M\). Such maps form the [[differential-geometry/category-of-symplectic-manifolds|category of symplectic manifolds]], and its isomorphisms are [[differential-geometry/symplectomorphism|symplectomorphisms]].

Given \(H\in C^\infty(M)\), nondegeneracy determines the [[differential-geometry/hamiltonian-vector-field|Hamiltonian vector field]] \(X_H\) from the house convention
\[
\iota_{X_H}\omega=dH.
\]
Its flow preserves \(\omega\). More generally, [[differential-geometry/symplectic-isotopy|symplectic isotopies]] are generated by time-dependent vector fields whose contractions with \(\omega\) are closed; [[differential-geometry/hamiltonian-isotopy|Hamiltonian isotopies]] require those one-forms to be exact.

## Examples

The standard form on \(\mathbb R^{2n}\) is \(\sum_i dq_i\wedge dp_i\). Every cotangent bundle carries its [[differential-geometry/canonical-symplectic-form-cotangent|canonical symplectic form]]. A [[differential-geometry/kahler-manifold|Kähler manifold]] is symplectic after retaining its fundamental form and forgetting the metric and complex structure. A closed two-form may fail to be symplectic if it is degenerate; the zero form is the simplest example.
