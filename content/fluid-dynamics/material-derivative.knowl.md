For a velocity field \(u\), the **material derivative** is
\[
D_t=\partial_t+u\cdot\nabla.
\]
It combines explicit time change with the [[fluid-dynamics/transport-operator|spatial transport operator]]. For a differentiable scalar field \(q\) and a [[fluid-dynamics/particle-trajectory|particle trajectory]], the chain rule gives
\[
\frac{d}{dt}q(t,X(t))=(D_tq)(t,X(t)).
\]
For Cartesian vector components, the derivative acts componentwise.

## Moving bases

When a vector is expressed in a position-dependent basis, differentiating the vector also differentiates the basis. Cylindrical component equations therefore contain curvature terms in addition to applying \(\partial_t+u_r\partial_r+(u_\theta/r)\partial_\theta+u_z\partial_z\) to each component.
