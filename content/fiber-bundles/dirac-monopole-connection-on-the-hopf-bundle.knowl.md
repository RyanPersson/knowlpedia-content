On the [[fiber-bundles/hopf-fibration-s3s2-as-a-principal-u-bundle|Hopf bundle]] \(S^3\subset\mathbb C^2\to\mathbb{CP}^1\cong S^2\), with right action \(z\cdot e^{it}=e^{it}z\), the **standard Dirac monopole connection** is the \(i\mathbb R\)-valued [[fiber-bundles/connection-1-form-on-a-principal-bundle|connection form]]
\[
\omega=\bar z_1\,dz_1+\bar z_2\,dz_2\quad\text{on }S^3.
\]
It is purely imaginary because \(d(|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2)=0\) on \(S^3\), is invariant under the circle action, and evaluates to \(it\) on the fundamental vector generated by \(it\in i\mathbb R\). Its horizontal spaces are \(\ker\omega\).

## Local gauge potentials

Identify the base with spherical coordinates \((\theta,\varphi)\) so that local sections away from the south and north poles are
\[
s_N=(\cos(\theta/2),e^{i\varphi}\sin(\theta/2)),\qquad
s_S=(e^{-i\varphi}\cos(\theta/2),\sin(\theta/2)).
\]
Write \(s_N^*\omega=iA_N\) and \(s_S^*\omega=iA_S\), with real-valued potentials. Then
\[
A_N=\frac{1-\cos\theta}{2}\,d\varphi,\qquad
A_S=-\frac{1+\cos\theta}{2}\,d\varphi.
\]
On the overlap, \(s_S=s_Ne^{-i\varphi}\), so the connection transformation law gives \(A_S=A_N-d\varphi\). The phase \(e^{-i\varphi}\) is a globally defined transition function there; \(\varphi\) itself is only a local angular coordinate. These potentials extend smoothly to their respective poles.

## Curvature and normalization

The curvature on the base is \(iF\), where
\[
F=dA_N=dA_S=\frac12\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\varphi,\qquad
\frac1{2\pi}\int_{S^2}F=1.
\]
The nonzero flux proves that \(F\) is not exact. With the Chern–Weil convention \(c_1=\frac{i}{2\pi}F_\nabla\), the associated tautological line bundle has \(c_1=-[F/(2\pi)]\); its dual has first Chern number \(+1\). Calling the monopole charge \(+1\) here refers to the displayed real-flux normalization.

The curvature class alone does not specify this standard connection: every connection on the same Hopf bundle has the same normalized cohomology class, while its curvature form can vary by an exact form.

## Holonomy and higher charges

For a positively oriented latitude loop at \(\theta=\theta_0\), the right-action horizontal-lift convention above gives the holonomy phase
\[
\exp\!\left(-i\oint A_N\right)
=\exp\!\left(-i\pi(1-\cos\theta_0)\right).
\]
Its exponent is minus half the enclosed solid angle. Reversing the loop orientation reverses this sign.

Taking the \(n\)th tensor power of the associated line bundle gives local potentials \(nA_N,nA_S\), transition \(e^{-in\varphi}\), and real curvature \(nF\). Its real flux is \(n\), and its first Chern number under the stated convention is \(-n\).
