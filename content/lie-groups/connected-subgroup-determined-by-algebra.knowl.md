Let \(G\) be a [[fiber-bundles/lie-group|Lie group]]. If \(H_1,H_2\subseteq G\) are connected [[lie-groups/lie-subgroup|immersed Lie subgroups]] and
\[
\mathrm{Lie}(H_1)=\mathrm{Lie}(H_2)\subseteq \mathfrak g,
\]
then \(H_1=H_2\).

Equivalently, every [[lie-groups/lie-subalgebra|Lie subalgebra]] \(\mathfrak h\subseteq\mathfrak g\) determines at most one connected immersed Lie subgroup of \(G\) with Lie algebra \(\mathfrak h\).

## Why this is true
One convenient way to see the mechanism is via the [[lie-groups/exponential-map-lie-group|exponential map]]. A connected Lie subgroup \(H\) with Lie algebra \(\mathfrak h\) is generated by \(\exp(U\cap\mathfrak h)\) for any sufficiently small neighborhood \(U\) of \(0\). Thus two connected subgroups with the same \(\mathfrak h\) are generated by the same one-parameter subgroups.

This uniqueness is one half of the [[lie-groups/lie-correspondence|Lie correspondence]] between connected subgroups and subalgebras; existence typically uses integrability of left-invariant distributions or the [[lie-groups/lies-third-theorem|third theorem of Lie]].
