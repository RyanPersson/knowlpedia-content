A **Fuchsian group** is a [[lie-groups/discrete-subgroup|discrete subgroup]] \(\Gamma\le\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb R)\), with the usual [[lie-groups/projective-special-linear-lie-group|Lie-group topology]]. It acts on the upper half-plane \(\mathcal H=\{z:\operatorname{Im}z>0\}\) by
\[
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\!\cdot z=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}.
\]
Matrices differing by sign give the same orientation-preserving hyperbolic isometry.

## Scope

Finite covolume, torsion-freeness, and [[lie-groups/arithmetic-fuchsian-group|arithmeticity]] are extra conditions. The definition here includes elementary and finite groups.

For example, the group generated by \(z\mapsto z+1\) is Fuchsian. Its quotient has infinite hyperbolic area: on a vertical strip of width \(1\), the area integral \(\int_0^1\int_0^1 y^{-2}\,dy\,dx\) diverges. Thus discreteness alone does not imply finite covolume.
