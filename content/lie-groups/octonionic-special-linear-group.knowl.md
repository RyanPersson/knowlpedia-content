Every traceless \(2\times2\) octonionic matrix acts as a real-linear
endomorphism of \(\mathbb O^2\cong\mathbb R^{16}\). The **octonionic special
linear [[lie-groups/lie-algebra|Lie algebra]]**
\(\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb O)\) is the
[[lie-groups/lie-subalgebra|Lie subalgebra]] of
\(\mathfrak{gl}_{16}(\mathbb R)\) generated by all these endomorphisms. The
connected closed subgroup of \(GL(16,\mathbb R)\) with this Lie algebra is
denoted \(SL_2(\mathbb O)\).

## Identification with a spin group

There are Lie algebra and [[fiber-bundles/lie-group|Lie group]] isomorphisms
\[
\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb O)\cong\mathfrak{so}(9,1),
\qquad
SL_2(\mathbb O)\cong\operatorname{Spin}(9,1).
\]
Its [[lie-groups/maximal-compact-subgroup-real-reductive-group|maximal compact
subgroup]] is \(\operatorname{Spin}(9)\). The action on
\(\mathbb O^2\) restricts to the real 16-dimensional spin representation of
that compact subgroup.

## Why this is not a naive matrix group

The real-linear maps represented by octonionic matrices are not closed under
commutators if one tries to compute using ordinary octonionic matrix
multiplication: nonassociativity creates additional endomorphisms. Thus
\(SL_2(\mathbb O)\) is defined through the generated real Lie algebra, not as
the set of octonionic matrices with a naive determinant equal to one.

## Geometric action

The group preserves the positive cone and
[[nonassociative-algebra/octonionic-two-by-two-determinant|determinant]] on
[[nonassociative-algebra/octonionic-spin-factor|\(H_2(\mathbb O)\)]]. It also
acts conformally on octonionic lines and
preserves the class of
[[complex-analysis/octonionic-plurisubharmonic-function|octonionic
plurisubharmonic functions]].
