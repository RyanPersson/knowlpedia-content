Let \(\mathfrak g\) be the [[lie-groups/lie-algebra|Lie algebra]] of a
[[lie-groups/real-reductive-lie-group|real reductive Lie group]], and choose a
nondegenerate invariant symmetric [[linear-algebra/bilinear-form|bilinear form]] \(B\) that is negative on the
compact central directions and positive on the split central directions. A
**Cartan involution** is a
[[lie-groups/lie-algebra-automorphism|Lie algebra automorphism]]
\(\theta:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g\) satisfying \(\theta^2=1\) such that
\[
B_\theta(X,Y)=-B(X,\theta Y)
\]
is positive definite. On the semisimple derived algebra one may take \(B\) to
be the [[lie-groups/killing-form|Killing form]]. The qualification concerning the center is necessary
because the Killing form of a [[lie-groups/reductive-lie-algebra|reductive Lie algebra]] is degenerate there.

## Eigenspaces and geometry

Writing \(\mathfrak k\) and \(\mathfrak p\) for the \(+1\) and \(-1\)
eigenspaces gives the
[[lie-groups/cartan-decomposition-real-reductive-lie-algebra|Cartan
decomposition]] \(\mathfrak g=\mathfrak k\oplus\mathfrak p\).
The form \(B_\theta\) makes these eigenspaces orthogonal. At group level,
\(\theta\) integrates to an involutive automorphism whose fixed subgroup is
maximal compact under the standard global hypotheses.

## Existence and uniqueness

Every real reductive Lie algebra admits a Cartan involution. Any two are
conjugate by an [[algebra-groups/inner-automorphism|inner automorphism]] generated by the noncompact directions.
Consequently the compact subalgebra \(\mathfrak k\), although not canonical as
a literal subspace without a choice, is unique up to inner automorphism. This
is why constructions made from a chosen \(\theta\) usually have
choice-independent isomorphism types.

## Matrix example and terminology

For \(\mathfrak{gl}_n(\mathbb R)\), the map
\(\theta(X)=-X^{\mathsf T}\) is a Cartan involution. Its fixed algebra is
\(\mathfrak{so}(n)\), and its \(-1\)-eigenspace consists of symmetric
matrices. A Cartan involution is not a [[lie-groups/cartan-subalgebra|Cartan subalgebra]] and should not be
confused with complex conjugation defining an arbitrary real form; it singles
out a compact real form after complexification.
