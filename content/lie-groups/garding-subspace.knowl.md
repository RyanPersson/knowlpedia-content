Let \(G\) be a finite-dimensional [[fiber-bundles/lie-group|Lie group]] with left [[harmonic-analysis/haar-measure|Haar measure]], and let \((\pi,\mathcal H)\) be a [[lie-groups/strongly-continuous-unitary-representation|strongly continuous unitary representation]]. For \(f\in C_c^\infty(G)\), define
\[
\pi(f)v=\int_G f(g)\pi(g)v\,dg.
\]
The **Gårding subspace** is
\[
\mathcal H_G=\operatorname{span}\{\pi(f)v:f\in C_c^\infty(G),\ v\in\mathcal H\}.
\]
Thus it is the image span of the smooth compactly supported convolution algebra under the [[harmonic-analysis/integrated-form-unitary-representation|integrated representation]]. Its elements are called Gårding vectors. The subspace is \(G\)-invariant, dense in \(\mathcal H\), and contained in the smooth-vector space.

## Smoothing mechanism

Differentiation may be moved from the [[fiber-bundles/orbit-map|orbit map]] to the test function:
\[
d\pi(X)\pi(f)v=-\pi(X_{\mathrm{right}}f)v
\]
with the sign and invariant [[fiber-bundles/vector-field|vector field]] determined by the convolution convention. Since every iterated derivative of \(f\) is again smooth and compactly supported, \(\pi(f)v\) is a [[lie-groups/smooth-vector-unitary-representation|smooth vector]]. This is the representation-theoretic analogue of regularization by convolution.

## Density and factorization

A smooth approximate identity \(f_j\) supported near the identity satisfies \(\pi(f_j)v\to v\), which proves density; this is the [[lie-groups/garding-density-theorem|Gårding density theorem]]. The deeper [[lie-groups/dixmier-malliavin-factorization-theorem|Dixmier–Malliavin factorization theorem]] strengthens the inclusion \(\mathcal H_G\subseteq\mathcal H^\infty\) to equality.

## Conventions and scope

Some authors call \(\mathcal H_G\) the Gårding domain and reserve “Gårding space” for a completed locally convex version. The algebraic span is essential: the set of individual vectors \(\pi(f)v\) need not itself be closed under addition in an obvious single-factor form. The same construction works for continuous representations on suitable complete [[functional-analysis/locally-convex-space|locally convex spaces]], with the integral interpreted there.
