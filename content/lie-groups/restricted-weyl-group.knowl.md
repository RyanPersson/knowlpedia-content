Let \(G\) be a connected
[[lie-groups/real-reductive-lie-group|real reductive Lie group]], let \(K\) be
a [[lie-groups/maximal-compact-subgroup-real-reductive-group|maximal compact subgroup]],
and let \(\mathfrak a\) be a maximal abelian subspace of the
noncompact part of a Cartan decomposition. The
**restricted Weyl group** is
\[
W(G,\mathfrak a)=N_K(\mathfrak a)/Z_K(\mathfrak a),
\]
where the numerator normalizes \(\mathfrak a\) under the adjoint action and
the denominator fixes it pointwise. It acts faithfully on \(\mathfrak a\) and
on the [[lie-groups/restricted-root-system|restricted root system]]. The group
is finite and is generated by the reflections in the hyperplanes orthogonal
to the restricted roots.

## Chambers and orbit representatives

The reflecting hyperplanes cut \(\mathfrak a\) into Weyl chambers, and the
restricted Weyl group acts simply transitively on the chambers. A closed
positive chamber is a fundamental domain for this action. Consequently many
conjugacy questions in \(G\) reduce to finite symmetry on \(\mathfrak a\);
for example, the Cartan decomposition parametrizes double cosets \(K\backslash
G/K\) by a closed chamber. See
[Knapp, Chapter VI, §§4–5 and Chapter VII, §3](https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~aknapp/books/beyond2.html).

## Comparison with the complex Weyl group

The restricted roots are the nonzero restrictions to \(\mathfrak a\) of
complex roots, and distinct complex roots may have the same restriction.
Accordingly, \(W(G,\mathfrak a)\) need not be the full
[[lie-groups/weyl-group|Weyl group]] of the complexified [[lie-groups/lie-algebra|Lie algebra]]. It is
the symmetry group adapted to the real form
and is often called the **little Weyl group**.

## Example

For \(G=\operatorname{SL}(n,\mathbb R)\), take \(\mathfrak a\) to be the
trace-zero diagonal matrices and \(K=\operatorname{SO}(n)\). Permutation
matrices, adjusted when necessary to have determinant \(1\), normalize
\(\mathfrak a\); after quotienting by the diagonal centralizer, one obtains
\(W(G,\mathfrak a)\cong S_n\), acting by permuting diagonal entries.
