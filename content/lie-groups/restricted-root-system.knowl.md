Let \(\mathfrak g=\mathfrak k\oplus\mathfrak p\) be the
[[lie-groups/cartan-decomposition-real-reductive-lie-algebra|Cartan
decomposition]] of a real [[lie-groups/reductive-lie-algebra|reductive Lie algebra]], and let
\(\mathfrak a\subseteq\mathfrak p\) be maximal abelian. For
\(\alpha\in\mathfrak a^*\), set
\[
\mathfrak g_\alpha=\{X\in\mathfrak g:
[H,X]=\alpha(H)X\text{ for every }H\in\mathfrak a\}.
\]
The **restricted root system** is
\[
\Sigma(\mathfrak g,\mathfrak a)
=\{\alpha\in\mathfrak a^*\setminus\{0\}:\mathfrak g_\alpha\ne0\}.
\]
Its elements are restricted roots, and
\(m_\alpha=\dim\mathfrak g_\alpha\) is the root multiplicity. Unlike an
ordinary reduced [[lie-groups/root-system|root system]], \(\Sigma\) may contain
both \(\alpha\) and \(2\alpha\).

## Root-space decomposition

The commuting operators \(\operatorname{ad}(H)\), \(H\in\mathfrak a\), are
simultaneously diagonalizable over \(\mathbb R\), giving
\[
\mathfrak g=\mathfrak g_0\oplus
\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Sigma}\mathfrak g_\alpha.
\]
Choosing a positive subsystem \(\Sigma^+\) defines the nilpotent algebra
\(\mathfrak n=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Sigma^+}\mathfrak g_\alpha\), the key
ingredient in the
[[lie-groups/iwasawa-decomposition|Iwasawa decomposition]].

## Weyl group and chambers

The [[lie-groups/restricted-weyl-group|restricted Weyl group]] is
\[
W(\mathfrak g,\mathfrak a)=N_K(\mathfrak a)/Z_K(\mathfrak a).
\]
It is generated by the reflections associated with indivisible restricted
roots and acts on \(\mathfrak a\). The [[topology/connected-component|connected components]] of the complement
of the root hyperplanes are Weyl chambers. Their closures parametrize the
radial part in the \(KAK\) decomposition.

## Example and comparison

For \(\mathfrak{sl}_n(\mathbb R)\), take \(\mathfrak a\) to be the diagonal
trace-zero matrices. The restricted roots are
\(\alpha_{ij}(H)=H_i-H_j\), each with multiplicity one, so the system is of
type \(A_{n-1}\). For other real forms, several complex roots can restrict to
the same functional, producing higher multiplicities or a nonreduced system.
