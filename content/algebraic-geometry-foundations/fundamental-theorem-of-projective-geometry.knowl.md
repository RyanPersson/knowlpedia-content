Let \(V\) and \(W\) be [[linear-algebra/vector-space|vector spaces]] of dimension at least \(3\) over fields \(k\) and \(\ell\). If a bijection
\[
f:\mathbb P(V)\longrightarrow\mathbb P(W)
\]
maps every [[algebraic-geometry-foundations/projective-line|projective line]] onto a projective line, then there are a field isomorphism \(\sigma:k\to\ell\) and a bijective \(\sigma\)-[[linear-algebra/semilinear-map|semilinear map]] \(T:V\to W\) such that
\[
f([v])=[T(v)].
\]
The map \(T\) is unique up to multiplication by a nonzero scalar in \(\ell\), and \(\sigma\) is unique.

## Collineation form

A bijection that maps projective lines onto projective lines is called a **collineation**. For a fixed \(k\)-vector space \(V\) of dimension at least \(3\), the theorem says that every collineation belongs to the [[algebraic-geometry-foundations/projective-semilinear-group|\(\operatorname{P\Gamma L}(V)\)]]. Those induced by genuinely [[linear-algebra/linear-map|linear maps]] form the subgroup \(\operatorname{PGL}(V)\).

The statement can equivalently require preservation of all projective subspaces or preservation of incidence in both directions. Once projective lines are preserved, higher-dimensional projective subspaces are preserved because they are generated by points and lines.

## Why projective dimension one is excluded

If \(\dim V=2\), then \(\mathbb P(V)\) is itself a projective line and has no proper projective lines on which to test incidence. Every permutation of its points is line-preserving in that vacuous sense, and most permutations do not arise from semilinear maps. Thus the hypothesis \(\dim V\ge3\), equivalently projective dimension at least \(2\), is essential.
