A **Huber pair** \((A,A^+)\) consists of a topological ring \(A\) admitting an
open subring with a finitely generated
[[algebra-topological/ideal-of-definition|ideal of definition]], together with an
open integrally closed subring \(A^+\subseteq A^\circ\) of power-bounded
elements.  Its **adic spectrum**

\[
\operatorname{Spa}(A,A^+)
\]

is the set of [[shared-foundations/equivalence-class|equivalence classes]] of
continuous valuations on \(A\) that are
bounded by \(1\) on \(A^+\), equipped with the topology generated by rational
subsets and with Huber's structure presheaf.

An **adic space** is a
[[algebraic-geometry-foundations/locally-ringed-space|locally ringed space]]
locally isomorphic to affinoid adic spectra for which the structure
presheaves are sheaves.

## Why two rings appear

The ring \(A\) records analytic functions, while \(A^+\) records an integral
structure.  Different choices of \(A^+\) can give the same broad analytic
geometry but different integral data.

## Relation to other geometries

Adic spaces include the spaces associated with Tate rigid-analytic varieties
and generic fibers of
[[algebraic-geometry-foundations/formal-scheme|formal schemes]]. Unlike a
[[algebra-commutative/prime-spectrum|scheme spectrum]], their points
are valuations of many ranks, so specialization and integral information are
visible simultaneously.

[[algebraic-geometry-foundations/perfectoid-space|Perfectoid spaces]] are
special adic spaces whose affinoid rings satisfy a strong Frobenius condition.
