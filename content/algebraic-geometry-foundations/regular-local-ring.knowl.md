A [[algebra-commutative/noetherian-ring|Noetherian]] [[algebra-commutative/local-ring|local ring]] \((R,\mathfrak m)\) is **regular** if its maximal ideal can be generated by \(\dim R\) elements. Equivalently,

\[
\dim_{R/\mathfrak m}(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2)=\dim R,
\]

where \(\dim R\) is the [[algebra-commutative/krull-dimension|Krull dimension]] of \(R\), and \(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\) is viewed as an \(R/\mathfrak m\)-[[linear-algebra/vector-space|vector space]]. The Noetherian hypothesis is part of this convention. For example, \(k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]_{(x_1,\ldots,x_n)}\) is regular of dimension \(n\).

This is the local dimension-theoretic notion used to define a regular scheme and to describe nonsingular fibers.
