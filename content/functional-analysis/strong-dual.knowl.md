Let \(E\) be a Hausdorff [[functional-analysis/locally-convex-space|locally convex space]] with [[functional-analysis/topological-dual|topological dual]] \(E'\). The **strong dual** \(E'_\beta\), also written \(E'_b\) or \((E',\beta(E',E))\), is \(E'\) equipped with the locally convex topology of [[real-analysis/uniform-convergence|uniform convergence]] on [[functional-analysis/bounded-subset-tvs|bounded subsets]] of \(E\). It is generated by the seminorms
\[
p_B(\varphi)=\sup_{x\in B}\lvert\varphi(x)\rvert,
\]
where \(B\) ranges over bounded subsets of \(E\). Each \(p_B\) is finite because a continuous linear functional sends bounded sets to bounded sets.

## Relation to other dual topologies

The [[functional-analysis/weak-star-topology|weak-star topology]] \(\sigma(E',E)\) is uniform convergence only on finite subsets of \(E\), so it is no finer than the strong topology. If \(E\) is normed, every bounded set is contained in a scalar multiple of its unit ball; consequently the strong dual topology is exactly the usual dual-norm topology. For a general locally convex space, no single bounded set need control all others.

## Boundedness governs the topology

Two locally convex topologies on the same [[linear-algebra/vector-space|vector space]] that have the same bounded subsets induce the same strong topology on their common continuous dual. This dependence on bounded sets makes strong duals natural for [[functional-analysis/distribution|distributions]]. In particular, \(\mathcal S'(\mathbb R^n)\) is commonly given the strong dual topology of the [[functional-analysis/schwartz-space|Schwartz space]], which is finer than [[real-analysis/pointwise-convergence|pointwise convergence]] on [[functional-analysis/test-function-space|test functions]].

## Conventions and cautions

The word **strong** here concerns uniform convergence on bounded subsets of a locally convex space. It should not be confused with the [[operator-algebras/strong-operator-topology|strong operator topology]] on an operator algebra. Authors may write \(E'_b\), \(E'_\beta\), or simply \(E'\) when the strong topology is understood; the last notation is ambiguous unless the surrounding text declares its dual-topology convention.
