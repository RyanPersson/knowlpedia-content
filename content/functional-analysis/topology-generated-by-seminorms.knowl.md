Let \(E\) be a [[linear-algebra/vector-space|vector space]] and let \(\mathcal P\) be a family of [[convex-analysis/seminorm|seminorms]] on \(E\). The **topology generated by \(\mathcal P\)** has a neighborhood basis at \(0\) consisting of the sets
\[
U(p_1,\ldots,p_n;\varepsilon)
=\{x\in E:p_j(x)<\varepsilon\text{ for }1\leq j\leq n\},
\]
where \(p_1,\ldots,p_n\in\mathcal P\), \(n<\infty\), and \(\varepsilon>0\). Neighborhoods at \(x\) are translates \(x+U\). This is the coarsest vector topology making every \(p\in\mathcal P\) continuous, and it makes \(E\) a [[functional-analysis/locally-convex-space|locally convex space]]. It is Hausdorff exactly when the seminorms separate points.

## Convergence

A net \(x_\alpha\) converges to \(x\) in the generated topology exactly when
\[
p(x_\alpha-x)\longrightarrow0
\qquad\text{for every }p\in\mathcal P.
\]
Sequences suffice only when the topology is first countable, so nets cannot generally be replaced by sequences for an uncountable seminorm family.

## Hausdorffness and metrizability

The topology is Hausdorff precisely when
\[
\bigcap_{p\in\mathcal P}\ker p=\{0\}.
\]
If a countable family \((p_n)\) generates the topology, then it is metrizable by, for example,
\[
d(x,y)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{-n}
\frac{p_n(x-y)}{1+p_n(x-y)}.
\]
Metrizability does not imply completeness; that is a separate condition on Cauchy nets or sequences.

## Comparison and examples

If \(\mathcal P\subseteq\mathcal Q\), then the topology generated by \(\mathcal Q\) is finer than the one generated by \(\mathcal P\). A single norm generates the usual norm topology. Evaluation seminorms generate pointwise-convergence topologies, while suprema of derivatives on [[topology/compact-set|compact sets]] generate the standard topologies on spaces of smooth functions.
