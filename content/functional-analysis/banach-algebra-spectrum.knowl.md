Let \(A\) be a complex unital [[functional-analysis/banach-algebra|Banach algebra]]
with identity \(1\), and let \(a\in A\). The **spectrum of \(a\) in \(A\)** is
\[
\sigma_A(a)=\{\lambda\in\mathbb C:\lambda 1-a
\text{ is not invertible in }A\}.
\]
Here invertibility means being a
[[functional-analysis/banach-algebra-invertible-element|Banach-algebra
invertible element]].
Its complement \(\rho_A(a)=\mathbb C\setminus\sigma_A(a)\) is the [[functional-analysis/banach-algebra-resolvent|resolvent set]]
of \(a\). The ambient algebra is part of the notation because invertibility, and
therefore the spectrum, can change when \(a\) is viewed in a larger algebra. For
a nonunital Banach algebra, the spectrum is defined in its
[[operator-algebras/unitization|unitization]], with the same formula.

## Compactness and spectral radius

The spectrum is a nonempty compact subset of \(\mathbb C\). Its maximum modulus
is the spectral radius
\[
r(a)=\max_{\lambda\in\sigma_A(a)}|\lambda|
     =\lim_{n\to\infty}\|a^n\|^{1/n}
     =\inf_{n\geq 1}\|a^n\|^{1/n}.
\]
The nonemptiness assertion uses completeness and complex scalars; it can fail
for real Banach algebras unless one passes to a complexification. The displayed
limit is the [[functional-analysis/banach-algebra-spectral-radius-formula|
Banach-algebra spectral-radius formula]].

## The \(C^*\)-algebra case

If \(A\) is a unital [[operator-algebras/cstar-algebra|\(C^*\)-algebra]] and
\(a\) is [[operator-algebras/self-adjoint-element|self-adjoint]], then
\(\sigma_A(a)\subseteq\mathbb R\). If \(a\) is normal, then
\(r(a)=\|a\|\); this equality need not hold for an arbitrary element.
Moreover, the [[operator-algebras/continuous-functional-calculus|continuous
functional calculus]] identifies the unital \(C^*\)-algebra generated by a
normal \(a\) with the continuous functions on \(\sigma_A(a)\).

## Ambient-algebra conventions

**Warning.** Spectra are preserved by unital injective homomorphisms between
\(C^*\)-algebras, but not by arbitrary embeddings of Banach algebras. In
particular, an element may acquire additional inverses after the ambient
algebra is enlarged. For a nonunital algebra, authors sometimes record whether
zero is included separately; the unitization convention above fixes that
choice.
