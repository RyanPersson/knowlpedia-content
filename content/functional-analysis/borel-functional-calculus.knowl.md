Let \(T\) be a self-adjoint operator, possibly unbounded, or a bounded normal
operator on a [[linear-algebra/hilbert-space|Hilbert space]], with [[functional-analysis/projection-valued-measure|spectral
measure]] \(E_T\). In the unbounded self-adjoint case, this measure is supplied
by the
[[functional-analysis/spectral-theorem-unbounded-self-adjoint|spectral
theorem]]. For a complex Borel function \(f\) on \(\sigma(T)\), its **Borel
functional calculus** is
\[
f(T)=\int_{\sigma(T)} f(z)\,dE_T(z).
\]
If \(f\) is unbounded, this [[functional-analysis/spectral-integral|spectral integral]] has domain
\[
\left\{\xi:\int_{\sigma(T)}|f(z)|^2\,d\langle E_T(z)\xi,\xi\rangle<\infty\right\}.
\]
For bounded \(f\), it is a bounded operator on the whole Hilbert space. Functions equal outside an \(E_T\)-null set determine the same operator.

## Algebraic and convergence properties

On bounded Borel functions, \(f\mapsto f(T)\) is a unital \(*\)-homomorphism, modulo \(E_T\)-null functions, and
\[
\lVert f(T)\rVert=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{E_T}|f|.
\]
Bounded [[real-analysis/pointwise-convergence|pointwise convergence]] passes to strong-operator convergence by dominated convergence for the scalar measures \(\langle E_T(\,\cdot\,)\xi,\xi\rangle\). For unbounded functions, sums and products require domain control; identities from the bounded calculus cannot be transferred without taking the appropriate closures.

## Relation to continuous functional calculus

For bounded normal \(T\), the continuous calculus sends \(C(\sigma(T))\) into the norm-closed \(C^*\)-algebra generated by \(T\) and the identity. The bounded Borel calculus is generally larger: its values lie in the [[operator-algebras/von-neumann-algebra|von Neumann algebra]] generated by the spectral projections. In particular, [[probability/characteristic-function-probability|characteristic functions]] of Borel sets produce projections that need not belong to the norm-closed algebra. This is the essential strengthening beyond [[operator-algebras/continuous-functional-calculus|continuous functional calculus]].

## Self-adjoint and normal cases

For self-adjoint \(T\), the [[functional-analysis/projection-valued-measure|spectral measure]] is supported on \(\mathbb R\), and real-valued \(f\) produce self-adjoint operators. For normal \(T\), the measure lives on \(\mathbb C\), and complex conjugation corresponds to adjunction. Taking \(f(z)=z\) recovers \(T\), including its domain when \(T\) is unbounded; taking \(f=1_B\) gives the [[functional-analysis/spectral-projection|spectral projection]] for \(B\).
