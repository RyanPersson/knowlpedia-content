Let \(A\) be a unital \(C^*\)-algebra and let \(a\in A\) be normal, meaning \(a^*a=aa^*\). On the [[functional-analysis/banach-algebra-spectrum|spectrum]] \(\sigma_A(a)\), the **continuous functional calculus** is the unique unital isometric \(*\)-homomorphism
\[
\Phi_a\colon C(\sigma_A(a))\longrightarrow A
\]
that sends the coordinate function \(z\mapsto z\) to \(a\). Its image is the [[operator-algebras/commutative-cstar-algebra|commutative \(C^*\)-algebra]] \(C^*(1,a)\), and one writes \(f(a)=\Phi_a(f)\). Thus algebraic operations, involution, and uniform limits of functions become the corresponding operations on elements of \(A\).

## Fundamental consequences

For every \(f\in C(\sigma_A(a))\),
\[
\|f(a)\|=\max_{\lambda\in\sigma_A(a)}|f(\lambda)|
\quad\text{and}\quad
\sigma_A(f(a))=f(\sigma_A(a)).
\]
The first identity is the norm formula; the second is [[operator-algebras/spectral-mapping-theorem|spectral mapping]]. For self-adjoint \(a\), real-valued functions give self-adjoint elements. In particular, nonnegative continuous functions construct positive square roots and [[real-analysis/absolute-value|absolute values]].

## Nonunital algebras

If \(A\) is nonunital, one applies the unital calculus in its [[operator-algebras/unitization|unitization]] \(\widetilde A\). For \(a\in A\), the element \(f(a)\) lies in \(A\) whenever \(f(0)=0\). Equivalently, the nonunital algebra generated by \(a\) corresponds to the continuous functions on \(\sigma_{\widetilde A}(a)\) that vanish at \(0\). This condition must not be omitted when \(0\) belongs to the unitized spectrum.
