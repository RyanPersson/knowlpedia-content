A [[operator-algebras/cstar-algebra|\(C^*\)-algebra]] \(A\) is **unital** if
there is an element \(1_A\in A\) such that
\[
1_Aa=a1_A=a\qquad(a\in A).
\]
The identity is unique. Except in the zero algebra, it is self-adjoint,
positive, and has norm one. Unitality is part of the ambient algebraic
structure, not a choice of an [[operator-algebras/approximate-identity|approximate identity]]. A
[[operator-algebras/star-homomorphism|\(*\)-homomorphism]] between unital
\(C^*\)-algebras need not preserve their identities unless it is explicitly
required to be unital.

## Consequences of having a unit

In a unital \(C^*\)-algebra, invertibility and the spectrum of \(a\) are defined
internally using \(1_A\). The continuous functional calculus for a normal
element is unital, and scalar multiples \(\lambda1_A\) provide a canonical
copy of \(\mathbb C\) when \(A\neq0\). A closed ideal of a unital algebra may
still be nonunital; it has a unit precisely when it is generated by a central
projection.

## Unitization

Every nonunital \(C^*\)-algebra \(A\) admits a unitization \(\widetilde A\)
containing \(A\) as a codimension-one closed ideal and fitting into
\[
0\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow\widetilde A\longrightarrow\mathbb C
\longrightarrow0.
\]
This construction lets one discuss spectra, invertibility, and unitary
elements without pretending that the adjoined identity lies in \(A\).
Different concrete embeddings may supply larger units, so the canonical
unitization and a multiplier algebra should not be conflated.

## Examples and conventions

The algebra \(C(X)\) is unital for compact Hausdorff \(X\), with constant
function \(1\), whereas \(C_0(X)\) is unital exactly when \(X\) is compact.
The compact operators on an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space are nonunital.
Some authors regard the zero algebra as unital with \(1=0\), while others
require \(1\neq0\); statements using \(\|1_A\|=1\) implicitly exclude the zero
case [Murphy, §2.1](https://doi.org/10.1016/C2009-0-22289-6).
