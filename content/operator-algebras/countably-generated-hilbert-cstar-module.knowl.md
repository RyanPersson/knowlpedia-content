Let \(A\) be a [[operator-algebras/cstar-algebra|\(C^*\)-algebra]] and let
\(E\) be a right
[[operator-algebras/hilbert-cstar-module|Hilbert \(A\)-module]]. The module
\(E\) is **countably generated** if there is a sequence
\((x_n)_{n\geq1}\subseteq E\) such that
\[
E=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{x_na:n\geq1,\ a\in A\},
\]
where the closure is taken in the Hilbert-module norm. “Generated” therefore
means topologically generated as a right \(A\)-module, not algebraically
generated by finitely supported combinations. A finite generating family is
allowed by repeating entries or appending zeros.

## Equivalent compact-operator criterion

The module \(E\) is countably generated precisely when its algebra
\(\mathcal K(E)\) of
[[operator-algebras/compact-operator-hilbert-module|compact Hilbert-module operators]] has a countable
[[operator-algebras/approximate-identity|approximate identity]]. This
converts a module generation condition into the \(\sigma\)-unitality of a
\(C^*\)-algebra
[Lance, Chapter 2].

## Stabilization

The [[operator-algebras/kasparov-stabilization-theorem|Kasparov stabilization theorem]] says that every countably generated \(E\) satisfies
\[
E\oplus H_A\cong H_A,
\]
where \(H_A\) is the
[[operator-algebras/standard-hilbert-cstar-module|standard Hilbert \(A\)-module]]. Thus countable generation is the exact smallness hypothesis
that permits \(E\) to occur as an orthogonally complemented submodule of one
standard module.

## Examples and scope

Every \(A^n\) and \(H_A=\ell^2(A)\) is countably generated. A closed
submodule of a countably generated Hilbert module need not be countably
generated without hypotheses on \(A\). Countable generation also does not
mean that \(E\) possesses a countable
[[linear-algebra/orthonormal-basis|orthonormal basis]]: Hilbert
\(C^*\)-modules generally need not admit such bases.
