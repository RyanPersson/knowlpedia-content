Let \(A\) be a [[operator-algebras/cstar-algebra|\(C^*\)-algebra]] and
\(B\subseteq A\) a
[[operator-algebras/hereditary-cstar-subalgebra|hereditary \(C^*\)-subalgebra]]. The subalgebra \(B\) is **full in \(A\)** if it is
contained in no proper
[[operator-algebras/closed-two-sided-ideal|closed two-sided ideal]] of \(A\).
Equivalently, the ideal generated by \(B\) is all of \(A\):
\[
\overline{\operatorname{span}}\,ABA=A.
\]
Fullness is therefore an ambient property of the inclusion \(B\subseteq A\);
the same \(C^*\)-algebra embedded hereditarily in two different ambient
algebras may be full in one and not in the other.

## Full corners

If \(p\) is a [[operator-algebras/projection-cstar-algebra|projection]] in
\(A\), or suitably in its
[[operator-algebras/multiplier-algebra|multiplier algebra]], then \(pAp\) is
hereditary. It is a **full corner** exactly when \(p\) is a full projection, meaning
\(\overline{\operatorname{span}}\,ApA=A\). Every full corner is thus a full
hereditary subalgebra, but a general hereditary subalgebra need not be
presented by a projection belonging to \(A\).

## Morita-theoretic significance

A full hereditary \(C^*\)-subalgebra \(B\) is
[[operator-algebras/strong-morita-equivalence|strongly Morita equivalent]]
to \(A\), with the completion of \(BA\) providing the equivalence module.
Under \(\sigma\)-unital hypotheses, the Brown–Green–Rieffel theorem further
relates this equivalence to stable isomorphism
[Brown–Green–Rieffel, Theorem 1.2].

## Examples and non-examples

For a nonzero projection \(p\in\mathcal K(H)\), the corner
\(p\mathcal K(H)p\) is full because the
[[operator-algebras/compact-operator-cstar-algebra|compact-operator algebra]] is simple.
By contrast, if \(A=A_1\oplus A_2\), then \(A_1\oplus0\) is hereditary but
not full: the ideal it generates is only \(A_1\oplus0\).
