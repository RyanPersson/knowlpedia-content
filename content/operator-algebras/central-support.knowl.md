Let \(p\) be a projection in a
[[operator-algebras/von-neumann-algebra|von Neumann algebra]] \(M\). The
**central support** of \(p\), denoted \(c_M(p)\), is the least projection
\(z\) in the
[[operator-algebras/center-of-von-neumann-algebra|center]] \(Z(M)\) such that
\(p\leq z\). It exists because arbitrary infima of projections exist in
\(M\), and it is given by
\[
c_M(p)=\bigwedge\{z\in Z(M):z\text{ is a projection and }p\leq z\}.
\]
Thus \(c_M(p)=1\) means that no nonzero central summand of \(M\) is
orthogonal to \(p\); one then says that \(p\) has full central support.
It depends on the ambient algebra, which is recorded by the subscript.

## Equivalent descriptions

The central support is also the join of the unitary conjugates of \(p\):
\[
c_M(p)=\bigvee_{u\in\mathcal U(M)}upu^*.
\]
Equivalently, the ultraweakly closed [[algebra-rings/two-sided-ideal|two-sided ideal]] generated by \(p\) is
\(Mc_M(p)\). These descriptions express that central support records every
central summand reached by \(p\), while forgetting its size inside each
summand.

## Basic properties

Central support is monotone: \(p\leq q\) implies
\(c_M(p)\leq c_M(q)\). It is invariant under unitary conjugacy and, more
generally, under
[[operator-algebras/murray-von-neumann-equivalence|Murray–von Neumann equivalence]].
For a family of projections,
\[
c_M\left(\bigvee_i p_i\right)=\bigvee_i c_M(p_i).
\]
If \(z\) is already central, then \(c_M(z)=z\).

## Factors and direct sums

In a factor, every nonzero projection has central support \(1\), because the
only central projections are \(0\) and \(1\). In a direct sum
\(M=\bigoplus_\alpha M_\alpha\), the central support of
\(p=(p_\alpha)\) is the central projection whose \(\alpha\)-component is
\(1\) exactly when \(p_\alpha\neq0\). Central support therefore detects which
factor summands occur, not the ranks of the components.
