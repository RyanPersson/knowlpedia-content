Let \(X\) be a
[[operator-algebras/cstar-correspondence|\(C^*\)-correspondence]] over
\(A\), with left action \(\varphi_X\), and write \(\mathcal K_A(X)\) for its
[[operator-algebras/compact-operator-hilbert-module|compact module
operators]]. Define
\[
J_X=\varphi_X^{-1}(\mathcal K_A(X))\cap(\ker\varphi_X)^\perp.
\]
A
[[operator-algebras/toeplitz-representation-cstar-correspondence|Toeplitz
representation]] \((t,\pi)\) is **Cuntz–Pimsner covariant** when
\[
\pi(a)=t^{(1)}(\varphi_X(a))\qquad(a\in J_X),
\]
where \(t^{(1)}\) is its representation of the compact module operators. The
**Cuntz–Pimsner algebra** \(\mathcal O_X\) is the universal
\(C^*\)-algebra generated by such a covariant representation. Here
\((\ker\varphi_X)^\perp=\{a\in A:ab=0\text{ for every }b\in\ker\varphi_X\}\).
This is Katsura’s definition for an arbitrary correspondence.

## Covariance ideal and quotient

The ideal \(J_X\) isolates precisely the part of \(A\) on which the left
action is compact while avoiding the kernel that would make the covariance
relation collapse coefficient elements. Equivalently,
\(\mathcal O_X\) is the quotient of the
[[operator-algebras/toeplitz-pimsner-algebra|Toeplitz–Pimsner algebra]]
\(\mathcal T_X\) by the ideal generated by
\[
i_A(a)-i_X^{(1)}(\varphi_X(a)),\qquad a\in J_X.
\]
This formulation extends the construction to noninjective left actions.

## Standard examples

For \(A=\mathbb C\) and \(X=\mathbb C^d\) with \(2\leq d<\infty\), covariance
adds \(\sum_{j=1}^d S_jS_j^*=1\) to the Toeplitz relations, producing the
Cuntz algebra \(\mathcal O_d\). A correspondence obtained from an automorphism
of \(A\) yields a crossed product by \(\mathbb Z\), with the orientation of
the automorphism determined by the chosen module convention. These examples
motivated Pimsner’s construction.

## Conventions and scope

**Warning.** Older “Cuntz–Pimsner algebra” conventions may assume that
\(\varphi_X\) is injective, acts by [[linear-algebra/compact-operator|compact operators]], or impose covariance
on a different ideal. Under those extra hypotheses the formulas agree in
their common range, but for a general correspondence the covariance ideal
must be stated. A relative Cuntz–Pimsner algebra imposes the relation only on
a chosen ideal contained in \(J_X\); it is not automatically \(\mathcal O_X\).
