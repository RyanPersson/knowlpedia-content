A [[operator-algebras/cstar-algebra|\(C^*\)-algebra]] \(A\) is
**\(\sigma\)-unital** if it has a sequential
[[operator-algebras/approximate-identity|approximate identity]]: there are
positive contractions \(e_1,e_2,\ldots\in A\) such that
\[
\lVert e_n a-a\rVert\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{and}\quad
\lVert a e_n-a\rVert\longrightarrow0
\]
for every \(a\in A\). Equivalently, \(A\) has a countable approximate
identity; a finite approximate identity may be repeated to form a sequence.
The prefix \(\sigma\) expresses this countability condition and does not refer
to a topology on \(A\).

## Equivalent characterizations

A \(C^*\)-algebra is \(\sigma\)-unital exactly when it contains a
[[operator-algebras/strictly-positive-element|strictly positive element]]
\(h\). In that case,
[[operator-algebras/continuous-functional-calculus|continuous functional calculus]] gives a canonical sequential approximate identity
\[
e_n=f_n(h),\qquad f_n(t)=\min\{nt,1\}.
\]
Conversely, a rapidly weighted sum of the terms of a countable approximate
identity produces a strictly positive element. These equivalences are
standard consequences of approximate-unit theory.

## Examples and permanence

Every unital \(C^*\)-algebra is \(\sigma\)-unital, using the constant sequence
\(1_A\). Every separable \(C^*\)-algebra is \(\sigma\)-unital, although the
converse fails: an unital algebra can be nonseparable. For a
[[topology/locally-compact-space|locally compact]]
[[topology/hausdorff-space|Hausdorff space]] \(X\), \(C_0(X)\) is
\(\sigma\)-unital exactly when \(X\) is \(\sigma\)-compact. If \(H\) is a
separable [[linear-algebra/hilbert-space|Hilbert space]], the finite-rank
projections onto an increasing sequence of finite-dimensional subspaces form
an approximate identity for
[[operator-algebras/compact-operator-cstar-algebra|\(K(H)\)]].

The property need not pass to arbitrary ideals. If \(D\) is an uncountable
discrete space and \(D^+\) its one-point compactification, then \(C(D^+)\) is
unital, but its ideal \(C_0(D)\) is not \(\sigma\)-unital. In contrast, a
[[operator-algebras/hereditary-cstar-subalgebra|hereditary subalgebra]]
generated by one positive element is automatically \(\sigma\)-unital.

## Why countability matters

Countable approximate identities allow arguments with sequences instead of
general nets. They are a standing hypothesis in much of multiplier-algebra,
Hilbert-module, and \(C^*\)-classification theory. The condition is weaker
than separability and stronger than merely possessing an approximate
identity, since every \(C^*\)-algebra has the latter.
