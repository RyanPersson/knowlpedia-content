Let \(E\) be a right [[operator-algebras/hilbert-cstar-module|Hilbert \(C^*\)-module]] over a \(C^*\)-algebra \(A\). Its **multiplier module** is
\[
M(E)=\mathcal L_A(A,E),
\]
the space of [[operator-algebras/adjointable-operator-hilbert-module|adjointable]] \(A\)-linear maps from the standard Hilbert \(A\)-module \(A\) to \(E\). Since \(\mathcal L_A(A)\cong M(A)\), the [[operator-algebras/multiplier-algebra|multiplier algebra]], \(M(E)\) is a Hilbert \(M(A)\)-module with
\[
(T\cdot m)(a)=T(m(a)),\qquad
\langle S,T\rangle_{M(A)}=S^*T.
\]
The canonical map \(E\to M(E)\) sends \(x\) to the multiplier \(a\mapsto xa\).

## Embedding and strict completion

The canonical map is an isometric \(A\)-module embedding and identifies
\(E\) with \(M(E)A\). Its image is strictly dense in \(M(E)\): if
\((e_\lambda)\) is an [[operator-algebras/approximate-identity|approximate identity]] for \(A\), then
\(T\cdot e_\lambda\) comes from \(E\) and converges strictly to \(T\).
The strict topology is generated by the seminorms
\[
T\longmapsto\lVert T(a)\rVert,\qquad
T\longmapsto\lVert T^*(x)\rVert
\]
for \(a\in A\) and \(x\in E\).

## Examples and structure

For the standard module \(E=A\), one recovers \(M(E)=M(A)\). If \(A\) is
unital, evaluation at \(1_A\) identifies \(M(E)\) with \(E\), so the
construction adds no new vectors. For a full \(E\), the multiplier module is
full over \(M(A)\), and adjointable operators on \(E\) extend uniquely to
strictly continuous adjointable operators on \(M(E)\).

## Conventions and scope

The construction is most useful for nonunital coefficient algebras and should
not be confused with the multiplier algebra of the \(C^*\)-algebra
\(\mathcal K_A(E)\), although the two are closely related. Some sources
reserve “multiplier module” for full Hilbert modules or describe it as a
maximal essential extension; fullness is not needed for the displayed
definition.
