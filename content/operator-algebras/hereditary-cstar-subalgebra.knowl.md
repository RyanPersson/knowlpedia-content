Let \(B\subseteq A\) be a
[[operator-algebras/cstar-subalgebra|\(C^*\)-subalgebra]]. It is
**hereditary** if, whenever \(a\in A\) and \(b\in B\) are
[[operator-algebras/positive-element|positive elements]] satisfying
\[
0\leq a\leq b,
\]
then \(a\in B\). Thus \(B\) is downward closed inside the positive cone of the
ambient algebra \(A\). Heredity depends on the specified inclusion
\(B\subseteq A\), not only on the abstract isomorphism type of \(B\). Every
[[operator-algebras/closed-two-sided-ideal|closed two-sided ideal]] is
hereditary, but a hereditary subalgebra need not absorb multiplication by
arbitrary elements of \(A\).

## Equivalent characterizations

For a \(C^*\)-subalgebra \(B\subseteq A\), the following are equivalent:

1. \(B\) is hereditary;
2. \(BAB\subseteq B\); and
3. \(b^*ab\in B\) for every \(a\in A\) and \(b\in B\).

These formulations connect order in the positive cone with algebraic
compression. They also show that \(\overline{bAb}\) is a hereditary
\(C^*\)-subalgebra whenever \(b\in A_+\)
[Pedersen, §1.5](https://doi.org/10.1016/C2016-0-03431-9).

## Corners and generated hereditary subalgebras

If \(p\) is a [[operator-algebras/projection-cstar-algebra|projection]] in
\(A\), the corner
\[
pAp=\{pap:a\in A\}
\]
is hereditary. More generally, for \(S\subseteq A_+\), the hereditary
subalgebra generated by \(S\) is the smallest hereditary \(C^*\)-subalgebra
containing \(S\); for a single positive element \(h\), it is
\(\overline{hAh}\). Functional calculus gives the same subalgebra as the
closure of \(h^{1/2}Ah^{1/2}\).

Hereditary subalgebras correspond, in the bidual
[[operator-algebras/von-neumann-algebra|von Neumann algebra]] \(A^{**}\), to
open projections. This correspondence is a principal reason they are useful
for support, Morita equivalence, and the internal geometry of ideals
[Blackadar, §II.3](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-28517-2).

## Examples and distinctions

Every [[operator-algebras/closed-two-sided-ideal|closed two-sided ideal]]
\(I\triangleleft A\) is hereditary. The corner \(pM_n(\mathbb C)p\) is
hereditary in \(M_n(\mathbb C)\), but for a noncentral projection \(p\) it is
not an ideal. This demonstrates that heredity is strictly weaker than the
two-sided absorption property.

The scalar subalgebra \(\mathbb C1\subseteq M_n(\mathbb C)\), for \(n>1\), is
not hereditary: a nontrivial projection \(q\) satisfies
\(0\leq q\leq1\), yet \(q\notin\mathbb C1\). Hence being unital, closed, or
stable under functional calculus does not by itself imply heredity in the
ambient algebra.
