Let \(A\) and \(B\) be [[operator-algebras/cstar-algebra|\(C^*\)-algebras]].
For \(x=\sum_i a_i\otimes b_i\in A\odot B\), define
\[
\|x\|_{\max}=\sup_{\pi_A,\pi_B}
\left\|\sum_i\pi_A(a_i)\pi_B(b_i)\right\|,
\]
where the supremum runs over pairs of [[operator-algebras/cstar-representation|
\(*\)-representations]] on a common
[[linear-algebra/hilbert-space|Hilbert space]] whose ranges commute. This
finite supremum is the **maximal \(C^*\)-tensor norm**, and the completion
\(A\otimes_{\max}B\) is the **maximal \(C^*\)-tensor product**. It is the
largest [[operator-algebras/cstar-tensor-norm|\(C^*\)-tensor norm]] on
\(A\odot B\).

## Universal property

If \(\varphi:A\to M(C)\) and \(\psi:B\to M(C)\) are nondegenerate
\(*\)-homomorphisms with commuting ranges, there is a unique
\(*\)-homomorphism
\[
\varphi\mathbin{\cdot}\psi:A\otimes_{\max}B\longrightarrow M(C)
\]
satisfying
\((\varphi\mathbin{\cdot}\psi)(a\otimes b)=\varphi(a)\psi(b)\).
Conversely, representations of \(A\otimes_{\max}B\) yield commuting
representations of the factors. This is the precise sense in which the
maximal tensor product is universal.

## Comparison with the minimal product

Because \(\|x\|_{\min}\leq\|x\|_{\max}\), the identity on \(A\odot B\)
extends to a canonical surjective map
\[
A\otimes_{\max}B\longrightarrow
A\otimes_{\min}B.
\]
The target is the [[operator-algebras/minimal-cstar-tensor-product|minimal
\(C^*\)-tensor product]].
The map is injective exactly when the two canonical norms agree for this pair
\((A,B)\). Requiring agreement for every \(B\) defines a
[[operator-algebras/nuclear-cstar-algebra|nuclear \(C^*\)-algebra]].

For [[operator-algebras/matrix-cstar-algebra|matrix algebras]], and more
generally when one factor is nuclear, the canonical map is an isomorphism.
Without such a hypothesis its kernel may be nonzero, so the notation
\(A\otimes B\) is ambiguous.

## Functoriality and quotients

Every pair of \(*\)-homomorphisms \(\varphi:A\to C\) and \(\psi:B\to D\)
induces a \(*\)-homomorphism
\(\varphi\otimes_{\max}\psi:A\otimes_{\max}B\to
C\otimes_{\max}D\). Maximal tensoring also has a strong quotient property:
if \(A\to A/I\) is the quotient map, then the induced map onto
\((A/I)\otimes_{\max}B\) has kernel generated by \(I\odot B\). This behavior
contrasts with the injectivity property of the minimal product.

## Conventions and scope

**Warning.** “Maximal” refers to the largest \(C^*\)-norm, not to a maximal
ideal or maximal completion among arbitrary topological tensor products. The
Banach-space projective tensor norm is a different construction and should
not be treated as an interchangeable definition.
