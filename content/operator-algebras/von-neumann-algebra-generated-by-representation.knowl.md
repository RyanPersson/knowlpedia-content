Let \(\pi:A\to\mathcal B(H)\) be a
[[operator-algebras/cstar-representation|representation of a \(C^*\)-algebra]].
The **von Neumann algebra generated by \(\pi\)** is
\[
W^*(\pi(A))=\pi(A)''.
\]
By the [[operator-algebras/von-neumann-bicommutant-theorem|von Neumann
bicommutant theorem]], this is the weak-operator and strong-operator closure
of the unital \(*\)-algebra generated by \(\pi(A)\). It is the smallest von
Neumann subalgebra of \(\mathcal B(H)\) containing the represented image. If
\(\pi\) is nondegenerate, the identity already lies in the strong closure of
\(\pi(A)\), so \(W^*(\pi(A))\) is the weak or strong closure of \(\pi(A)\)
itself.

## Why the bicommutant is used

Taking a bicommutant automatically adjoins the identity and closes in the
relevant operator topologies. This matters for a degenerate representation:
the weak closure of \(\pi(A)\) can act as zero on a complementary subspace
and therefore need not contain \(I_H\), while \(\pi(A)''\) is unital. Adding
\(I_H\) before taking the weak closure gives the same algebra as the
bicommutant.

## Dependence on the representation

The construction depends on the representation, not only on the abstract
algebra \(A\). Different representations of the same \(C^*\)-algebra may
generate nonisomorphic
[[operator-algebras/von-neumann-algebra|von Neumann algebras]] or different normal
representation theories. The universal representation produces the
enveloping von Neumann algebra \(A^{**}\), whereas a particular quotient or
factor representation can generate a much smaller algebra.

## Role in representation theory

Properties formulated using weak limits become visible only after passing
from \(\pi(A)\) to \(W^*(\pi(A))\). A representation is
[[operator-algebras/factorial-representation|factorial]] when this generated
algebra is a factor.
[[operator-algebras/quasi-equivalent-representations|Quasi-equivalence of
representations]] asks whether their generated von Neumann algebras are
normally isomorphic in a way that agrees on the common \(C^*\)-algebra.
