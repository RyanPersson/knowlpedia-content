Let \(A\) be a nonzero [[operator-algebras/cstar-algebra|\(C^*\)-algebra]].
For every [[operator-algebras/state-cstar-algebra|state]]
\(\varphi\in S(A)\), choose its
[[operator-algebras/gns-construction|GNS representation]]
\((\pi_\varphi,H_\varphi,\xi_\varphi)\). The **universal representation** is
the [[operator-algebras/direct-sum-cstar-representations|direct sum]]
\[
\pi_u=\bigoplus_{\varphi\in S(A)}\pi_\varphi
\quad\text{on}\quad
H_u=\bigoplus_{\varphi\in S(A)}H_\varphi.
\]
It is a faithful
[[operator-algebras/nondegenerate-cstar-representation|nondegenerate
representation]]. Its unitary-equivalence class does not depend on the chosen
models of the GNS triples. “Universal” here
refers to simultaneously containing the cyclic representations generated by
states, not to an initial or [[algebra-category-theory/terminal-object|terminal object]] among all representations.

## Why it is faithful

States separate the positive elements of a \(C^*\)-algebra: for every nonzero
\(a\), some state is nonzero on \(a^*a\). The corresponding GNS summand then
acts nontrivially on \(a\), so \(\pi_u(a)\neq0\). This also gives a direct GNS
proof of the faithful-representation part of the
[[operator-algebras/gelfand-naimark-theorem|Gelfand–Naimark theorem]]
[Murphy, §§3.3–3.4].

## Enveloping von Neumann algebra

The [[operator-algebras/bicommutant|bicommutant]] \(\pi_u(A)''\) is
canonically isomorphic, as a
[[operator-algebras/von-neumann-algebra|von Neumann algebra]], to the bidual
\(A^{**}\). Under this identification the canonical map \(A\to A^{**}\)
agrees with \(\pi_u\), and every bounded functional on \(A\) extends uniquely
to a [[operator-algebras/normal-functional|normal functional]] on \(A^{**}\).
This is the universal enveloping von Neumann algebra construction
[Takesaki, vol. I, Chapter III].

## Scope of the sum

Some sources sum GNS representations over all [[operator-algebras/positive-linear-functional|positive functionals]], or choose
only enough states to separate points. Such sums can also be faithful, but
their multiplicities—and even their unitary-equivalence classes—need not agree
with \(\pi_u\). The definition here fixes the direct sum over all states. It
should not be confused with a universal \(C^*\)-algebra specified by generators
and relations.
