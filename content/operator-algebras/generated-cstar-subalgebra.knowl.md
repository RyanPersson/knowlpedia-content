Let \(A\) be a [[operator-algebras/cstar-algebra|\(C^*\)-algebra]] and
\(S\subseteq A\). The **\(C^*\)-subalgebra generated by \(S\)**, denoted
\(C^*(S)\), is the intersection of all
[[operator-algebras/cstar-subalgebra|\(C^*\)-subalgebras]] of \(A\) that
contain \(S\). Equivalently, it is the norm closure in \(A\) of the
algebraic \(*\)-subalgebra generated by \(S\). If generated subalgebras are
required to contain \(1_A\), one instead takes the closure of the unital
\(*\)-algebra generated by \(S\), commonly written \(C^*(S,1_A)\). Thus the
ambient unital convention is part of the notation.

## Universal property

The inclusion \(S\subseteq C^*(S)\) is minimal: any \(C^*\)-subalgebra
\(B\subseteq A\) containing \(S\) also contains \(C^*(S)\). Consequently, a
\(*\)-homomorphism defined on \(A\) is determined on \(C^*(S)\) by its values
on \(S\). This is an internal universal property relative to \(A\), not the
universal \(C^*\)-algebra on abstract generators and relations.

## Functional calculus and examples

For a normal element \(a\in A\), the generated algebra \(C^*(a,1_A)\) is
commutative and contains every element obtained from \(a\) by
[[operator-algebras/continuous-functional-calculus|continuous functional calculus]]. The diagonal matrix units generate the diagonal algebra in
\(M_n(\mathbb C)\), whereas all matrix units \(e_{ij}\) generate
\(M_n(\mathbb C)\). A merely algebraic \(*\)-algebra generated by a set may
fail to be norm closed and is then strictly smaller than \(C^*(S)\).

## Conventions and scope

When \(A\) is nonunital, \(C^*(S)\) is never made unital by adjoining an
external identity unless this is explicitly stated. When \(A\) is unital,
authors differ on whether “subalgebra” means “unital subalgebra”; the
notations \(C^*(S)\) and \(C^*(S,1_A)\) separate these conventions.
