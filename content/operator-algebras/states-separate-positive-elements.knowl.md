Let \(A\neq0\) be a complex
[[operator-algebras/cstar-algebra|\(C^*\)-algebra]], not necessarily unital,
and let \(S(A)\) be its [[operator-algebras/state-cstar-algebra|state space]].
The statement that **states separate positive elements** is
\[
\|a\|=\sup_{\varphi\in S(A)}\varphi(a)
\qquad(a\in A_+).
\]
In fact the supremum is attained: for every
[[operator-algebras/positive-element|positive element]] \(a\), some state
\(\varphi\) satisfies \(\varphi(a)=\|a\|\). Consequently, if
\(a,b\in A_+\) and \(\varphi(a)=\varphi(b)\) for every state, then \(a=b\).
Equivalently, a self-adjoint \(x\in A\) is positive exactly when
\(\varphi(x)\geq0\) for every state \(\varphi\).

## Proof mechanism

Apply the [[operator-algebras/continuous-functional-calculus|continuous functional calculus]] to the commutative algebra generated by \(a\). Evaluation
at the largest point of the spectrum gives a norm-one
[[operator-algebras/positive-linear-functional|positive functional]] whose
value at \(a\) is \(\|a\|\). Extending that functional to \(A\) without
increasing its norm produces a state. Thus states attain the norm of positive
elements.

To obtain the order criterion, if a self-adjoint \(x\) is not positive, its
negative spectral part yields a state with \(\varphi(x)<0\). Applying this to
\(a-b\) also shows that states separate arbitrary self-adjoint elements, not
only elements of \(A_+\).

## Norm consequences

For every \(x\in A\),
\[
\|x\|^2=\|x^*x\|
=\sup_{\varphi\in S(A)}\varphi(x^*x).
\]
For self-adjoint \(x\), one similarly has
\[
\|x\|=\sup_{\varphi\in S(A)}|\varphi(x)|.
\]
Thus states recover the norm even when \(A\) is originally presented
abstractly. These formulas underlie the isometry in the
[[operator-algebras/gns-construction|GNS construction]] and the universal
representation.

## Example and near-miss

For \(A=M_n(\mathbb C)\) and \(a\geq0\), a unit eigenvector for the largest
eigenvalue defines a [[operator-algebras/vector-state|vector state]] attaining
\(\|a\|\).

A single faithful state need not recover the norm of every positive element.
For example, normalized matrix trace assigns \(1/n\) to a rank-one projection,
although that projection has norm \(1\). The theorem requires the whole state
space.
