Let \(M\) be a
[[operator-algebras/von-neumann-algebra|von Neumann algebra]]. A projection
\(p\in M\) is an **abelian projection** if the corner \(pMp\), regarded as a
von Neumann algebra with unit \(p\), is abelian. Equivalently,
\[
xy=yx\qquad\text{for all }x,y\in pMp,
\]
or the [[operator-algebras/center-of-von-neumann-algebra|center]] of \(pMp\)
is all of \(pMp\). The definition concerns the compressed algebra, not merely
the one-dimensional algebra generated by \(p\). In particular, \(p\) need
not be central in \(M\), and an abelian projection can have nontrivial central
support.

## Relation to minimal projections

In a factor, a nonzero projection is abelian exactly when it is
[[operator-algebras/minimal-projection|minimal]]:
indeed, the center of \(pMp\) is scalar, so an abelian corner must equal
\(\mathbb Cp\). Outside factors, an abelian projection need not be minimal;
the commutative corner may contain many projections arising from its own
center.

## Type I criterion

A von Neumann algebra is type I precisely when every nonzero central
projection dominates a nonzero abelian projection. Equivalently, its central
summands are generated by abelian projections. For a factor, this reduces to
the existence of one nonzero abelian projection, leading to the form
\(B(H)\).

## Examples and nonexamples

Every projection in an [[operator-algebras/commutative-von-neumann-algebra|abelian von Neumann algebra]] is abelian. In \(B(H)\),
the abelian projections are exactly the projections of rank at most one.
By contrast, a type II factor has no nonzero abelian projections, even though
it has many nonzero [[operator-algebras/finite-projection|finite projections]].
Thus “abelian” and “finite” describe different features of a corner.
