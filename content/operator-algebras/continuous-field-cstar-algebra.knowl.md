Let \(X\) be a locally compact Hausdorff space. A **continuous field of \(C^*\)-algebras** over \(X\) consists of [[operator-algebras/cstar-algebra|\(C^*\)-algebras]] \(A_x\) and a \(*\)-algebra \(\Gamma\subseteq\prod_{x\in X}A_x\) of sections such that: each evaluation \(\Gamma\to A_x\) has dense range; \(x\mapsto\lVert s(x)\rVert\) is continuous for every \(s\in\Gamma\); and any section locally uniformly approximable by members of \(\Gamma\) belongs to \(\Gamma\). With the standard topology generated by these sections, this is equivalently an [[operator-algebras/upper-semicontinuous-cstar-bundle|upper-semicontinuous \(C^*\)-bundle]] whose norm is continuous. This knowl uses “continuous \(C^*\)-bundle” for that equivalent bundle presentation.

## Field and bundle presentations

The section axioms determine a topology on the disjoint union \(\bigsqcup_x A_x\). Fiberwise algebraic operations become continuous, and the norm is continuous on the total space. Conversely, the continuous sections of a continuous \(C^*\)-bundle recover a field satisfying the local approximation axiom. This equivalence is the reason the two terminologies are often interchanged [Nilsen, bundle realization](https://doi.org/10.1512/iumj.1996.45.1086).

The sections in \(\Gamma\) whose norm functions vanish at infinity form the [[operator-algebras/section-algebra-cstar-bundle|section algebra]] \(\Gamma_0(X,\mathcal A)\), which remembers the field as a \(C_0(X)\)-algebra. The continuity requirement is stronger than the upper-semicontinuity available for a general \(C_0(X)\)-algebra.

## Examples and non-examples

For a fixed \(C^*\)-algebra \(B\), the trivial field \(A_x=B\) has section algebra \(C_0(X,B)\). A locally trivial bundle with fiber \(B\) is another continuous field, although continuous fields need not be locally trivial and their fibers may change isomorphism type.

An upper-semicontinuous \(C^*\)-bundle for which some section has a discontinuous norm function is not a continuous field. It still has a valid section \(C^*\)-algebra, but fails the defining continuity condition.
