Let \(A\) be a [[operator-algebras/cstar-algebra|\(C^*\)-algebra]] and let
\(a\in A\) be a
[[operator-algebras/positive-element|positive element]]. There exists a unique
positive element \(b\in A\) satisfying \(b^2=a\). This element is the
**positive square root** of \(a\) and is denoted \(a^{1/2}\). It belongs to the
commutative \(C^*\)-subalgebra generated by \(a\) and is obtained from the
[[operator-algebras/continuous-functional-calculus|continuous functional
calculus]]
applied to \(t\mapsto\sqrt t\) on \(\sigma(a)\subseteq[0,\infty)\).
The adjective “positive” is essential: an element can have other, nonpositive
square roots.

## Basic properties

The construction satisfies
\[
\|a^{1/2}\|=\|a\|^{1/2},\qquad
(a^{1/2})^*a^{1/2}=a.
\]
It is order preserving: \(0\leq a\leq b\) implies
\(a^{1/2}\leq b^{1/2}\). Every \(*\)-homomorphism \(\pi:A\to B\) preserves the
construction, since uniqueness gives
\(\pi(a^{1/2})=\pi(a)^{1/2}\). The existence, uniqueness, and monotonicity
properties follow from functional calculus.

## Examples and a near-miss

For a positive matrix \(a=u\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n)u^*\),
\[
a^{1/2}=u\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{\lambda_1},\ldots,\sqrt{\lambda_n})u^*.
\]
For a nonnegative function \(f\in C_0(X)\), the positive square root is the
pointwise function \(\sqrt f\). A self-adjoint element with negative spectrum
has no positive square root, because the square of a positive element is
positive.

## Multiplicative caution

If positive \(a\) and \(b\) commute, then \(ab\) is positive and
\((ab)^{1/2}=a^{1/2}b^{1/2}\). Without commutativity, \(ab\) need not even be
self-adjoint, so this formula is generally meaningless as an identity of
positive square roots.
