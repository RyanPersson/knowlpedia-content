Fix generators \(\mathcal G\), relations \(\mathcal R\), and a choice of unital
or nonunital [[algebra-category-theory/category|category]] of
[[operator-algebras/cstar-algebra|\(C^*\)-algebras]]. A **universal
\(C^*\)-algebra** for this data is a \(C^*\)-algebra
\(C^*(\mathcal G\mid\mathcal R)\) with distinguished generators satisfying
\(\mathcal R\), such that every realization of the same relations in an
object \(B\) induces a unique
[[operator-algebras/star-homomorphism|\(*\)-homomorphism]]
\(C^*(\mathcal G\mid\mathcal R)\to B\) carrying each universal generator to
its realization. The chosen morphism class is part of the specification.
When it exists, the universal algebra is determined up to a unique
\(*\)-isomorphism preserving the generators.

## Existence and construction

Formal generators and relations first define an algebraic \(*\)-algebra. One
then takes the supremum of the operator seminorms arising from all admissible
\(*\)-representations and completes after quotienting by the common kernel.
Existence requires these seminorms to be finite on every element; in
generators-and-relations language this is typically ensured by admissibility
or explicit norm bounds. Arbitrary algebraic relations need not define a
nonzero, or even existent, universal \(C^*\)-algebra.

## Standard examples

The universal unital \(C^*\)-algebra generated by one unitary is
\(C(\mathbb T)\): a unitary \(u\) in any unital \(C^*\)-algebra determines a
unique unital \(*\)-homomorphism sending the coordinate function to \(u\).
The full group \(C^*\)-algebra is universal for unitary representations of a
group, while the Cuntz algebras are universal for isometries satisfying the
Cuntz relations. Each example depends on its stated morphism and unit
conventions.

## Conventions and scope

“Universal \(C^*\)-algebra” describes a representing property, not one single
algebra. In the unital category the comparison maps are required to preserve
the identity; in a nonunital category they are not. Some sources reserve
“universal enveloping \(C^*\)-algebra” for the \(C^*\)-completion of a
\(*\)-algebra. This notion is unrelated to universal algebra in the sense of
algebraic signatures and varieties, and “universal algebra” is therefore not
used here as an alias.
