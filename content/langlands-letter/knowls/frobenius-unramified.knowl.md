Let \(K/F\) be a finite [[algebra-fields-galois/galois-extension|Galois
extension]] of
[[langlands-letter/knowls/global-local-fields-completions|global fields]],
let \(v\) be a place of \(F\) unramified in \(K\), and choose \(w\mid v\).
The [[algebra-fields-galois/inertia-subgroup|inertia subgroup]] is then
trivial, and reduction gives an isomorphism from the
[[algebra-fields-galois/decomposition-group|decomposition group]]:

\[
D_w
\xrightarrow{\sim}
\operatorname{Gal}(\kappa(w)/\kappa(v)).
\]

The **arithmetic Frobenius** \(\operatorname{Frob}^{\mathrm{arith}}_w\) is
the element acting on the [[algebra-commutative/residue-field|residue field]] by

\[
x\longmapsto x^{q_v}.
\]

Its inverse is the **geometric Frobenius**
\(\operatorname{Frob}^{\mathrm{geom}}_w\).

## Conjugacy class

Changing \(w\) conjugates the decomposition subgroup and its Frobenius.
Consequently the [[algebra-groups/conjugacy-class|conjugacy class]]
\(\operatorname{Frob}_v\subset\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)\) is independent of
the chosen place above \(v\).

For an infinite
[[algebra-fields-galois/galois-extension|Galois extension]] or an
\(\ell\)-adic representation,
Frobenius is recorded in the appropriate finite quotient or as a conjugacy
class modulo the [[algebra-fields-galois/inertia-subgroup|inertia
subgroup]].

## Weil-group convention

For a
[[algebra-fields-galois/nonarchimedean-local-field|nonarchimedean local field]], the [[langlands/weil-group|Weil group]] maps onto
\(\mathbb Z\), generated by a chosen arithmetic or geometric Frobenius.
[[langlands/local-class-field-theory|Local class field theory]] likewise has
two reciprocal normalizations: a
[[algebra-commutative/dvr|uniformizer]] can map to arithmetic Frobenius or
its inverse.

## Langlands role

Unramified Galois parameters and
[[langlands/satake-parameter|Satake parameters]] are compared at Frobenius.
Changing convention inverts the Frobenius element and its eigenvalues, so
the
[[langlands-letter/knowls/euler-product-and-local-factor|local
\(L\)-factor]] formula must change with it.

## Relation to the letter

The letter's element \(\sigma\) is the Galois component of its unramified
class \(\alpha_p\). Reading its determinant formula requires fixing which
of the two Frobenius conventions \(\sigma\) denotes.
