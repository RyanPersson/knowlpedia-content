The six-sphere is the unit sphere
\[
S^6=\{x\in\mathbb R^7:\lVert x\rVert=1\}.
\]
A complex structure on this real six-manifold means a complex-manifold atlas of complex dimension three: each chart maps an open subset of \(S^6\) to an open subset of \(\mathbb C^3\), and all transition maps are holomorphic.

Such an atlas induces an almost-complex structure, a smooth bundle endomorphism \(J:TS^6\to TS^6\) satisfying \(J^2=-I\). The converse requires integrability: the almost-complex structure must locally arise from holomorphic coordinates. Thus an almost-complex structure is necessary but is not, by definition, enough.

## Open problem

Does \(S^6\) admit a complex-manifold structure?

The sphere does admit almost-complex structures, including a distinguished one arising from the octonions. The unresolved issue is whether any almost-complex structure on \(S^6\)—not necessarily the standard octonionic one—is integrable. The question is about existence; a positive answer would require constructing a compatible holomorphic atlas, while a negative answer would require obstructing every possible such atlas.

## Boundary

Among spheres, \(S^2\) has its familiar complex structure as the Riemann sphere. Standard topological constraints rule out almost-complex structures on most other spheres, leaving \(S^6\) as the exceptional unresolved case for an actual complex structure.
