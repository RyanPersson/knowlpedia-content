A semialgebraic subset of \(\mathbb R^n\) is a finite Boolean combination of sets cut out by polynomial equalities and strict inequalities. A function \(f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R\) is piecewise polynomial if there is a finite closed semialgebraic cover \(\mathbb R^n=S_1\cup\cdots\cup S_m\) and polynomials \(p_i\in\mathbb R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\) such that \(f(x)=p_i(x)\) on \(S_i\). Agreement on overlaps makes this a single well-defined function.

For finitely many real-valued functions, \(\sup\) and \(\inf\) are simply pointwise maximum and minimum. Thus an expression
\[
\sup_{1\le i\le r}\inf_{1\le j\le s_i}g_{ij}(x)
\]
is built from polynomial functions using finitely many pointwise maxima and minima.

## Conjecture

Every piecewise-polynomial function \(f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R\) admits a finite sup–inf representation by polynomials: there are finite families \(g_{ij}\in\mathbb R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\) for which
\[
f(x)=\sup_i\inf_j g_{ij}(x)
\]
for all \(x\in\mathbb R^n\).

Equivalently, the lattice generated by the polynomial functions under pointwise maximum and minimum contains every continuous piecewise-polynomial function. The closed-cover definition already forces continuity, since the local polynomial formulas agree wherever their pieces overlap.

## Known boundary

The conjecture is known in dimensions one and two. Higher-dimensional cases remain open and connect real algebraic geometry with the order structure of rings of functions.
