The **product topology** on a family of [[topology/topological-space|topological spaces]] \(\{(X_i,\mathcal T_i)\}_{i\in I}\) is the topology on \(\prod_{i\in I}X_i\) generated by the subbasis
\[
\{\pi_i^{-1}(U) : i\in I,\ U\in\mathcal{T}_i\},
\]
where \(\pi_i:\prod_{j\in I}X_j\to X_i\) is the \(i\)th projection.

## Equivalent characterizations

Equivalently, it is the coarsest topology on \(\prod_{i\in I}X_i\) making every projection \(\pi_i\) a [[topology/continuous-map|continuous map]].

## Remarks

In the case of two spaces \(X\times Y\), the sets \(U\times V\), with \(U\) open in \(X\) and \(V\) open in \(Y\), form a [[topology/basis-of-topology|basis]].

## Examples

- The usual topology on \(\mathbb R^n\) is the product topology of \(n\) copies of \(\mathbb R\).
- If \(X\) is discrete and \(Y\) is any space, the sets \(\{x\}\times V\), with \(V\) open in \(Y\), form a basis for \(X\times Y\).
- In an infinite product \(\prod_{i\in I}X_i\), a basic open set constrains only finitely many coordinates.
