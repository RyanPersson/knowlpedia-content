Let \(X\) be a [[topology/topological-space|topological space]]. For \(n\ge 0\), a **singular \(n\)-simplex** in \(X\) is a [[topology/continuous-map|continuous map]] \(\sigma:\Delta^n\to X\), where \(\Delta^n=\{(t_0,\ldots,t_n)\in\mathbb R^{n+1}:t_i\ge0,\sum_i t_i=1\}\). The group of singular \(n\)-chains is the free abelian group of finite formal integer sums of such simplices,
\[
C_n(X;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z[\{\sigma:\Delta^n\to X\text{ continuous}\}].
\]

For \(n\ge1\), let \(\delta_i:\Delta^{n-1}\to\Delta^n\) insert a zero in the \(i\)-th coordinate. Define
\[
\partial_n\sigma=\sum_{i=0}^n(-1)^i\,\sigma\circ\delta_i,\qquad \partial_0=0,
\]
and extend linearly. The identity \(\partial_{n-1}\partial_n=0\) makes
\[
\cdots\longrightarrow C_2(X;\mathbb Z)\xrightarrow{\partial_2}C_1(X;\mathbb Z)\xrightarrow{\partial_1}C_0(X;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow0
\]
a [[algebra-homological/chain-complex|chain complex]], called the **singular chain complex** of \(X\).

## Coefficients and maps

For an abelian group \(A\), singular chains with coefficients in \(A\) are \(C_n(X;A)=C_n(X;\mathbb Z)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}A\). A continuous map \(f:X\to Y\) sends \(\sigma\) to \(f\circ\sigma\), inducing a chain map \(C_\bullet(X;A)\to C_\bullet(Y;A)\).

## Reference

This construction is standard; see Allen Hatcher, *Algebraic Topology*, Chapter 2, [author-hosted book](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/ATpage.html).
