A **pasture** is a band \(P\) such that every nonzero element is a unit,
\(P^\times=P\setminus\{0\}\), and whose null ideal \(N_P\) is generated by its
three-term null relations under ideal operations and **fusion**:
\[
N_P=
\left\langle\!\left\langle
a+b+c\;\middle|\;a,b,c\in P,\ a+b+c\in N_P
\right\rangle\!\right\rangle.
\]
Here a band is a pointed commutative monoid whose null set is an ideal in its
semiring of formal sums and in which every element has a unique additive
inverse. The double brackets mean closure not only under addition and
multiplication by formal sums, but also under the fusion rule
\[
c+\alpha\in N_P,\quad -c+\beta\in N_P
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\alpha+\beta\in N_P.
\]
Thus a pasture is both a fusion band and an
[[algebra-hyperstructures/idyll|idyll]].

Morphisms are band morphisms: they preserve multiplication, \(0\), \(1\), and
null sums.

## Why three terms are singled out

Three-term Grassmann–Plücker relations govern weak matroids and cross-ratios.
The fusion closure derives higher-arity null relations by cancelling a term
against its additive inverse. Requiring all null relations to arise this way
produces a category in which universal pastures and matroid foundations can be
constructed by generators and relations.

## Relation to neighboring structures

- Fields, [[algebra-hyperstructures/partial-field|partial fields]], and
  [[algebra-hyperstructures/hyperfield|hyperfields]] have associated
  pastures.
- Every pasture has an associated tract, but not every tract is determined by
  three-term relations or is the tract underlying a pasture.
- A pasture admits an [[algebraic-geometry-foundations/ordered-blueprint|ordered-blueprint]] avatar with unique weak inverses.
- A pasture is **not** simply an arbitrary ordered blue field. Its band,
  field-like, and fusion-generation conditions are substantive.

The earlier presentation starts with a pointed multiplicative group and a
permutation-invariant set of three-term null relations. Closing those relations
under ideal operations and fusion gives the band presentation above.
