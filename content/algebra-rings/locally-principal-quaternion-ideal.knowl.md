Let \(K\) be a number field with \(R=\mathcal O_K\), and let \(\mathcal O\) be an [[algebra-rings/quaternion-order|order in a quaternion algebra]] \(B/K\). A right [[algebra-rings/quaternion-fractional-ideal|fractional \(\mathcal O\)-ideal]] \(I\) is **locally principal** if, for every nonzero prime \(\mathfrak p\) of \(R\),
\[
I_{\mathfrak p}=a_{\mathfrak p}\mathcal O_{\mathfrak p}
\quad\text{for some }a_{\mathfrak p}\in B^\times.
\]

Here \(I_{\mathfrak p}=R_{\mathfrak p}\otimes_RI\) and \(\mathcal O_{\mathfrak p}=R_{\mathfrak p}\otimes_R\mathcal O\) use [[algebra-commutative/localization-at-prime|localization at the prime]]. For left ideals the generator is placed on the right.

## Local versus global

A principal right ideal \(a\mathcal O\) is locally principal. The converse can fail: the local generators need not arise from one global element of \(B^\times\). The [[algebra-rings/quaternion-ideal-class-set|ideal class set]] records that obstruction.

For a [[algebra-rings/maximal-order|maximal quaternion order]] over a number ring, every full fractional one-sided ideal is locally principal. The fullness condition is necessary in split algebras.
