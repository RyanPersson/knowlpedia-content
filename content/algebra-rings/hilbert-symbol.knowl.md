Let \(F\) be a [[algebra-fields-galois/local-field|local field]] of characteristic different from two, and let \(a,b\in F^\times\). The **Hilbert symbol** \((a,b)_F\in\{+1,-1\}\) is \(+1\) if the [[algebra-rings/quaternion-algebra|quaternion algebra]] generated by \(i^2=a\), \(j^2=b\), \(ij=-ji\) is [[algebra-rings/split-quaternion-algebra|split]], and \(-1\) if it is a division algebra.

This page uses the sign-valued convention. The similar notation \((a,b)_F\) for the quaternion algebra itself denotes an algebra, not a sign.

## Square classes

Multiplying either parameter by a nonzero square does not change the symbol. It therefore descends to \(F^\times/F^{\times2}\) in each argument. The resulting pairing is symmetric and multiplicative in each variable.

Over \(\mathbb R\), the value is \(-1\) exactly when both parameters are negative. Over \(\mathbb C\), it is always \(+1\).

## The prime two matters

For odd integers \(a,b\),
\[
(a,b)_{\mathbb Q_2}=(-1)^{(a-1)(b-1)/4}.
\]
Thus \((-1,-1)_{\mathbb Q_2}=-1\), while \((-1,-1)_{\mathbb Q_p}=+1\) at every odd prime \(p\).

The sign is also distinct from the [[algebra-rings/quaternion-local-invariant|additive local Brauer invariant]], whose quaternion values are \(0\) and \(\tfrac12\) modulo \(\mathbb Z\).
