Given a [[shared-foundations/subset|subset]] \(A\subseteq R\) of a [[algebra-rings/ring|ring]] \(R\), the **two-sided ideal generated by \(A\)**, denoted \((A)\), is the smallest [[algebra-rings/ideal|two-sided ideal]] of \(R\) containing \(A\).

## Generators in a unital ring

If \(R\) is unital, \((A)\) consists of all finite sums
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n r_i a_i s_i,
\qquad a_i\in A,\quad r_i,s_i\in R,\quad n\ge0.
\]
The empty sum is zero. If \(R\) is also commutative, this reduces to finite sums \(\sum_i r_i a_i\). The smallest-ideal definition above also applies when \(R\) is nonunital.

## Remarks

When \(A=\{a\}\) is a singleton and \(R\) is commutative, \((A)\) is a [[algebra-rings/principal-ideal|principal ideal]].

## Examples

- In \(\mathbb Z\), the ideal generated by \(\{6,10\}\) is \((2)\).
- In \(k[x,y]\), the ideal generated by \(\{x,y\}\) is \((x,y)\).
- In \(M_2(k)\), the two-sided ideal generated by a nonzero matrix is all of \(M_2(k)\).
