**Proposition.** Let \(G\) be a finite [[algebra-groups/group|group]] with \(|G|=p^2\) for a prime \(p\). Then \(G\) is [[algebra-groups/abelian-group|abelian]].

## Proof

As a finite [[algebra-groups/p-group|\(p\)-group]], \(G\) has nontrivial center \(Z(G)\). If \(|Z(G)|=p^2\), then \(Z(G)=G\). Otherwise \(|Z(G)|=p\), so \(G/Z(G)\) has order \(p\) and is cyclic by [[algebra-groups/prime-order-cyclic|prime-order implies cyclic]]. A group whose quotient by its center is cyclic is abelian: if \(G/Z(G)\) is generated by \(gZ(G)\), write \(x=g^a z_1\) and \(y=g^b z_2\) with \(z_1,z_2\in Z(G)\); then \(xy=yx\). Thus \(G\) is abelian in either case.

## Remarks

A common strategy for classifying groups of small order is to show that the center is nontrivial and then pass to the quotient by the center.
