An **abelian group** is a [[algebra-groups/group|group]] \((G,\cdot)\) such that, for all \(a,b\in G\),
\[
a\cdot b = b\cdot a.
\]

## Notation

Abelian groups are often written additively: the operation is \(+\), the identity is \(0\), and the inverse of \(a\) is \(-a\).

## Examples

- \((\mathbb Z,+)\) and \((\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z,+)\) are abelian groups.
- Every [[linear-algebra/vector-space|vector space]] is an abelian group under addition.
- The symmetric group \(S_3\) is not abelian.
