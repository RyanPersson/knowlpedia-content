For [[algebra-commutative/fractional-ideal|fractional ideals]] \(I,J\) of an integral domain \(R\) with [[algebra-rings/fraction-field|fraction field]] \(K\), their **product** is
\[
IJ=\left\{\sum_{\nu=1}^m x_\nu y_\nu:
 m\ge0,\ x_\nu\in I,\ y_\nu\in J\right\}\subseteq K.
\]

The empty sum is zero. Finite sums are essential: the set of individual products alone need not be an additive subgroup.

## Closure and algebraic laws

If \(dI\subseteq R\) and \(eJ\subseteq R\), then \(deIJ\subseteq R\). Nonzero elements of \(I\) and \(J\) have a nonzero product, so \(IJ\) is again a nonzero fractional ideal.

Multiplication is associative and commutative, with identity \(R\). On ordinary ideals it agrees with [[algebra-rings/product-of-ideals|ideal multiplication]]. [[algebra-commutative/invertible-fractional-ideal|Invertible fractional ideals]] are precisely the units of this [[algebra-groups/commutative-monoid|commutative monoid]].

## A calculation

Over \(\mathbb Z\),
\[
\left(\frac23\mathbb Z\right)\left(\frac57\mathbb Z\right)
=\frac{10}{21}\mathbb Z.
\]
This product is not set intersection or set union.
