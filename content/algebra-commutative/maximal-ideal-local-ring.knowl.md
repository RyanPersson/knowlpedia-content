Let \(R\) be a [[algebra-rings/commutative-ring|commutative ring]]. Write \(R^\times\) for its group of units and set
\[
\mathfrak m := R\setminus R^\times
\]
for the set of nonunits. Then the following are equivalent:

1. \(R\) is a [[algebra-commutative/local-ring|local ring]].
2. The set \(\mathfrak m\) is an ideal of \(R\).

When these conditions hold, \(\mathfrak m\) is the unique maximal ideal of \(R\).

## Remarks

For \(R_{\mathfrak p}\), the [[algebra-commutative/localization-at-prime|localization at a prime]], the unique maximal ideal is \(\mathfrak pR_{\mathfrak p}\), and the associated [[algebra-commutative/residue-field|residue field]] is \(R_{\mathfrak p}/\mathfrak pR_{\mathfrak p}\).

This units-versus-maximal-ideal dichotomy is used in [[algebra-commutative/nakayama-lemma|Nakayama's lemma]].

## Examples

1. **\(\mathbb Z_{(p)}\).** In \(R=\mathbb Z_{(p)}\), a fraction \(\frac{a}{b}\), where \(p\nmid b\), is a unit if and only if \(p\nmid a\). Thus the maximal ideal is \(p\mathbb Z_{(p)}\).

2. **\(k[x]_{(x)}\).** In \(R=k[x]_{(x)}\), the units are exactly the fractions \(\frac{f}{g}\) with \(f(0)\neq 0\). Hence the maximal ideal is generated by \(x\).

3. **A field.** In a field \(k\), the only nonunit is \(0\), so \(\mathfrak m=(0)\).
