Let \(R\) be an integral domain with [[algebra-rings/fraction-field|fraction field]] \(K\). A **principal fractional ideal** is a [[algebra-commutative/fractional-ideal|fractional ideal]] of the form
\[
aR=\{ar:r\in R\},\qquad a\in K^\times.
\]

The generator need not lie in \(R\). The nonzero convention for fractional ideals is in force.

## Examples and equality

For \(R=\mathbb Z\), both \(2\mathbb Z\) and \(\tfrac12\mathbb Z\) are principal fractional ideals; only the first is an ordinary ideal contained in \(\mathbb Z\).

One has \(aR=bR\) exactly when \(a/b\) is a [[algebra-rings/unit|unit]] of \(R\). Moreover \((aR)(bR)=abR\), and \((aR)^{-1}=a^{-1}R\). Thus principal fractional ideals form a subgroup of the [[algebra-commutative/invertible-fractional-ideal|invertible fractional ideals]].

## Class groups

Quotienting invertible fractional ideals by this subgroup gives the [[algebra-commutative/picard-group|Picard group]] of the domain. For a [[algebra-fields-galois/ring-of-integers|ring of integers]] this recovers the [[algebra-fields-galois/ideal-class-group|ideal class group]].
