Let \((R,\mathfrak m)\) be a [[algebra-commutative/local-ring|local ring]], let \(M\) be a finitely generated \(R\)-module, and let \(k=R/\mathfrak m\) be the [[algebra-commutative/residue-field|residue field]]. The following are consequences of [[algebra-commutative/nakayama-lemma|Nakayama's lemma]]:

1. **Generators lift.**
   If \(m_1,\dots,m_r \in M\) map to elements whose images generate \(M/\mathfrak m M\) as a \(k\)-vector space, then \(m_1,\dots,m_r\) generate \(M\) as an \(R\)-module.

2. **Vanishing test.**
   If \(\mathfrak m M = M\), then \(M=0\). Equivalently, \(M=0\) if and only if \(M/\mathfrak m M=0\).

3. **Minimal number of generators.**
   The minimal number of \(R\)-module generators of \(M\) equals \(\dim_k(M/\mathfrak m M)\). In particular, a nonzero \(M\) is [[algebra-modules/cyclic-module|cyclic]] if and only if \(\dim_k(M/\mathfrak m M)=1\).

## Interpretation

These statements convert information modulo the maximal ideal into information about the whole module.

## Examples
1. **Free modules over a DVR-like local ring.**
   Let \(R=k[[t]]\) with \(\mathfrak m=(t)\), and take \(M=R^n\). Then
   \(M/\mathfrak m M \cong k^n\) has \(k\)-dimension \(n\), so \(R^n\) needs (and has) exactly \(n\) generators.

2. **A cyclic module detected mod \(\mathfrak m\).**
   With \(R=k[[t]]\) and \(\mathfrak m=(t)\), let \(M=R/(t^2)\). Then \(M/\mathfrak m M \cong k\), so \(\dim_k(M/\mathfrak m M)=1\), and the corollary says \(M\) is cyclic (generated by the class of \(1\)).

3. **Localizing the integers.**
   Let \(R=\mathbb Z_{(p)}\) with maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m=(p)\), and let \(M=(p^2)\) viewed as an \(R\)-module (an ideal). Then \(M/\mathfrak m M = (p^2)/(p^3)\) is \(1\)-dimensional over \(R/\mathfrak m \cong \mathbb F_p\), so \(M\) is generated by a single element (namely \(p^2\)).
