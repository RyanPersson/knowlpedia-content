Fix a primitive character \(\psi\). Form \(G_\psi(x)\) from the [[analytic-number-theory/centered-prime-sum|centered prime sums]] for the characters generated by \(\psi\), with the positive weights specified below. For \(0<\theta<1\) and fixed \(\lambda>1\), assume that for every \(Y_0\) there is a \(Y\ge Y_0\) such that
\[
G_\psi(x)\le x^\theta \qquad (Y\le x\le Y^\lambda).
\]
The **windowed Landau criterion** in Alpöge’s argument concludes that the corresponding primitive \(L\)-functions have no zeros with real part greater than \(\theta\).

## Which sum is bounded

Let \(H\) consist of the powers of \(\psi\), using the primitive character inducing each power for its \(L\)-function. Give the zeta term \(G(x)\) weight \(1\), and the real part of each nontrivial term \(G(\chi,x)\) weight \(1/(3|H|+1)\). Positivity keeps the zeros of the target family visible in the combined expression.

## Why windows suffice

The cohomological estimates select the [[analytic-number-theory/scale-window|ranges of \(x\)]] available to the argument. Taking \(\theta\) arbitrarily close to \(1/2\) from above excludes zeros strictly to its right. The functional equations, applied to the characters and their conjugates, give the corresponding conclusion on the left.
