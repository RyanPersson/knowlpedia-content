In the Dirichlet setting, the **generalized Riemann hypothesis** (GRH) states that for every [[analytic-number-theory/primitive-dirichlet-character|primitive Dirichlet character]] \(\chi\), each zero of [[analytic-number-theory/dirichlet-l-function|\(L(s,\chi)\)]] in the [[analytic-number-theory/critical-strip|open strip]] \(0<\operatorname{Re}s<1\) lies on the [[analytic-number-theory/critical-line|critical line]] \(\operatorname{Re}s=\tfrac12\). The character of modulus \(1\) recovers the [[analytic-number-theory/riemann-hypothesis|Riemann hypothesis]].

## Other moduli

An imprimitive character is induced by a [[analytic-number-theory/primitive-dirichlet-character|primitive]] one. The [[analytic-number-theory/euler-factors-under-induction|change-of-modulus formula]] gives finitely many correction factors whose zeros have real part zero. This is why the open-strip assertion for primitive characters covers all moduli.
