A **prime power** is an integer \(p^k\) with \(p\) a [[analytic-number-theory/prime-number|prime number]] and \(k\) a positive [[shared-foundations/natural-numbers|natural number]]. For example, \(2,4,8\) and \(9\) are prime powers. The number \(12=2^2\cdot3\) has two distinct prime factors, so it falls outside this set.

## In a prime sum

A cutoff \(p^k\le x\) limits both \(p\) and \(k\). At \(x=10\) the eligible integers are \(2,3,4,5,7,8,9\). The [[analytic-number-theory/von-mangoldt-function|von Mangoldt weight]] assigns \(4\) and \(8\) the same weight as \(2\).
