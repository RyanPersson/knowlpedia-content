A **CM point** (or **special point**) of a Shimura variety is represented by a Hodge homomorphism that factors through an algebraic torus defined over \(\mathbb Q\). Equivalently, its Mumford–Tate group, the smallest rational algebraic group through which that homomorphism factors, is a torus. On a moduli space of polarized [[arithmetic-geometry/abelian-variety|abelian varieties]] such a point represents an abelian variety with complex multiplication.

## An example on the modular curve

The point represented by \(i\) in the upper half-plane corresponds to \(\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+i\mathbb Z)\), with [[arithmetic-geometry/complex-multiplication|multiplication by \(i\)]]. The post uses particular CM points modeled on powers of a CM elliptic curve. Other CM points can represent simple abelian varieties of higher dimension.
