An **isogeny** \(f:A\to B\) is a surjective homomorphism of [[arithmetic-geometry/abelian-variety|abelian varieties]] with finite kernel as a group scheme. Over \(\mathbb C\), it identifies \(B\) with the quotient of \(A\) by a finite subgroup. The phrase “up to isogeny” permits this finite difference.

## An elliptic example

Over \(\mathbb C\), multiplication by \(2\) on an elliptic curve \(E\) has kernel of order \(4\) and is an isogeny of degree \(4\). In general, the existence of an isogeny \(A\to B\) also gives one \(B\to A\), so that isogeny is an equivalence relation over the chosen base field.
