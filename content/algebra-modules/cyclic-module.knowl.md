A left \(R\)-[[algebra-modules/module|module]] \(M\) is **cyclic** if there exists \(m\in M\) such that
\[
M=Rm=\{rm:r\in R\}.
\]
## Equivalent characterizations

For a generator \(m\), the map \(R\to M\), \(r\mapsto rm\), is surjective with kernel equal to the [[algebra-modules/annihilator-element|annihilator]] \(\operatorname{ann}(m)\). Hence
\[
M\cong R/\operatorname{ann}(m)
\]
as left \(R\)-modules. Conversely, every quotient of the left regular module \(R\) is cyclic.

## Examples

- As a \(\mathbb Z\)-module, \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) is generated by \(1\bmod n\).
- Any principal left ideal \(Ra\) is generated by \(a\).
- The zero module is cyclic, generated by \(0\).
