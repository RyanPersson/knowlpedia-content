Let \(R\) be a commutative unital ring. An \(R\)-[[algebra-modules/module|module]] \(L\) is **invertible** if there is an \(R\)-module \(M\) and an isomorphism
\[
L\otimes_R M\cong R,
\]
where \(\otimes_R\) is the [[algebra-modules/tensor-product|tensor product]]. Thus \(L\) has an inverse for tensor multiplication of modules up to isomorphism.

## Local description

Equivalently, \(L\) is a finitely generated [[algebra-modules/projective-module|projective module]] with \(L_{\mathfrak p}\cong R_{\mathfrak p}\) for every [[algebra-rings/prime-ideal|prime ideal]] \(\mathfrak p\). The inverse is the dual \(L^\vee=\operatorname{Hom}_R(L,R)\), and evaluation gives \(L\otimes_RL^\vee\cong R\).

For a domain, an [[algebra-commutative/invertible-fractional-ideal|invertible fractional ideal]] is an example. It need not be generated by one element globally, even though it has one generator after localization at each prime.

## Picard group

The isomorphism classes of invertible modules form the [[algebra-commutative/picard-group|Picard group]] of \(R\). The identity class is represented by \(R\) itself.
