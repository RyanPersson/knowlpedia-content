Let \(M\) be a [[algebra-modules/module|module]] over a [[algebra-rings/commutative-ring|commutative ring]] \(R\). Its **exterior algebra**, also called its **Grassmann algebra**, is
\[
\bigwedge_R M
=T_R(M)\big/\langle x\otimes x:x\in M\rangle,
\]
where \(T_R(M)\) is the [[algebra-modules/tensor-algebra|tensor algebra]]. It is graded as \(\bigwedge_R M=\bigoplus_{d\geq0}\bigwedge^d_RM\), with \(M\) in degree one.

## Alternating universal property

The map \(M^d\to\bigwedge^d_RM\) given by
\[
(x_1,\ldots,x_d)\longmapsto x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge x_d
\]
is alternating and universal among alternating \(R\)-multilinear maps out of \(M^d\).

## Signs

The defining relation implies
\[
x\wedge y=-y\wedge x
\]
for degree-one elements over every commutative base ring. More generally, homogeneous elements satisfy \(u\wedge v=(-1)^{pq}v\wedge u\) for degrees \(p,q\). In characteristic \(2\), graded commutativity alone does not imply \(x\wedge x=0\), so the alternating relation remains part of the definition.

## Finite free case

If \(M\) is free of rank \(n\) with basis \(e_1,\ldots,e_n\), then the products \(e_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_d}\) with \(i_1<\cdots<i_d\) form a basis of \(\bigwedge^d_RM\). Hence \(\bigwedge_RM\) has rank \(2^n\).
