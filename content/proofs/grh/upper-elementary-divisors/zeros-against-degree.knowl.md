**Proposition.** Let \(T\) be a [[proofs/grh/upper-elementary-divisors/frobenius-cycle|Frobenius cycle]] for \((N,W,R)\), \(N\ge1\), and fix [[proofs/grh/upper-elementary-divisors/root-data|root data]] \((a,a',i)\). Let \(c\) be real weights of the variables with bounded increments, \(f\in R[x_{b,s}]\), \(\alpha\) an exponent and \(l\ge0\) an integer. Suppose

- (least) for every \(g\in\mathrm{SL}_2^{a,a'}(W)\), \(g\cdot f\in\mathrm{G}_{l+c\cdot\alpha}(F^{w},c)\) ([[proofs/grh/upper-elementary-divisors/gauss-filtration|Gauss filtration]]): every term of every translate has \(w\)-adic order plus \(c\)-weight at least \(l+c\cdot\alpha\);
- (attained) \(w^{l+1}\) does not divide the coefficient \(f_\alpha\) of \(x^{\alpha}\) in \(f\).

Then \(q\,\nu(\alpha)\le d(\alpha)\), where \(\nu\) and \(d\) are the zero and pole weights.

## Proof

Let \(b=l+c\cdot\alpha\), \(\rho\) the slope, \(p=\mathrm{S}(f)\in R[t][x]\), and \(\Phi\in(R/w^{l+1})[t]\) the reduction of the merged coefficient \(\Phi_\alpha(p)\). We show: (i) \(\deg\Phi\le d(\alpha)\); (ii) \(\Phi\ne0\); (iii) \(\Phi(t+\iota_i(r))\) is divisible by \(t^{\nu(\alpha)}\) for every \(r\in W\). [[proofs/grh/upper-elementary-divisors/stepanov-count|Stepanov's count]], applied to \(W\to R/w^{l+1}\), then gives \(\nu(\alpha)q\le\deg\Phi\le d(\alpha)\).

*Expansion at infinity.* Filter \(R[t][x]\) by the iterated Gauss filtration \(G^{\infty}\) in which \(w\) weighs \(1\), \(t\) weighs \(-\rho\), and a variable \(z\) weighs \(c(z)+\mathrm{pole}(z)\rho\). Then \(\mathrm{S}_0(z)\in G^{\infty}_{c(z)}\) for every variable: \(\mathrm{S}_0(Y_i)=X_i\) has weight \(c(X_i)+q\rho=c(Y_i)\), and \(t^{\mathrm{pole}(z)}z\) has weight \(c(z)\). The cross terms lie in \(G^{\infty}_{c(z)+1/20}\): for \(z=Y_s\), \(s\ne i\), the term \(\varepsilon_sX_s\) has weight \(c(X_s)+qP_s\rho\ge c(X_s)+2\ge c(Y_s)+1\); for \(z=X_s\), \(\varepsilon_st^{P_s}Y_s\) has weight \(c(Y_s)-P_s\rho\ge c(X_s)+\tfrac12-\tfrac15\). By [[proofs/grh/upper-elementary-divisors/substitution-respects-gauss-filtration|the substitution lemma]] (hypothesis (least) for \(g=1\)), \(p\in G^{\infty}_{b}\) and \(p-\mathrm{S}_0(f)\in G^{\infty}_{b+1/20}\). Since \(X_i\) and \(Y_i\) have the same weight in \(G^{\infty}\), the monomial \(t^{j}x^{\bar\alpha}\) has weight \(c\cdot\alpha+(d(\alpha)-j)\rho\). Hence the coefficient of \(t^{j}\) in \(\Phi_\alpha(p)\) is divisible by \(w^{e}\) with \(e\ge b-c\cdot\alpha-(d(\alpha)-j)\rho=l+(j-d(\alpha))\rho\); for \(j>d(\alpha)\) this exceeds \(l\), so the coefficient vanishes modulo \(w^{l+1}\): (i). For \(j=d(\alpha)\), the same computation for \(p-\mathrm{S}_0(f)\) gives order \(\ge l+\tfrac1{20}>l\), so modulo \(w^{l+1}\) the coefficient of \(t^{d(\alpha)}\) in \(\Phi\) is that of \(\Phi_\alpha(\mathrm{S}_0(f))\). Now \(\mathrm{S}_0(x^{\beta})=t^{d(\beta)}x^{\bar\beta}\), and \(\beta\) is determined by \((\bar\beta,d(\beta))\): \(\bar\beta\) forgets only how the exponent splits between \(X_i\) and \(Y_i\), and moving one unit from \(Y_i\) to \(X_i\) raises the pole weight by \(q\ne0\). So that coefficient is exactly \(f_\alpha\), nonzero modulo \(w^{l+1}\): (ii).

*Expansion at a finite point.* Fix \(r\in W\) and let \(\mathrm{sh}\) be the substitution \(t\mapsto t+\iota_i(r)\) in the coefficients. Filter by \(G^{0}\): \(w\) weighs \(1\), \(t\) weighs \(\rho\), and \(z\) weighs \(c(z)-\mathrm{zero}(z)\rho\). Let \(\theta(z)=\pm\mathrm{S}(\tilde z)\), where \(\tilde z\) is \(z\) with \(X_s\) and \(Y_s\) exchanged and the sign is that of \(g_r\) at \(z\). A case check with the bounds \(P_s\rho\le\frac15\), \(qP_s\rho\ge2\), \(q\rho=c(Y_i)-c(X_i)\) shows \(\theta(z)\in G^{0}_{c(z)}\) for all \(z\) (e.g. \(\theta(X_i)=\pm X_i\) of weight \(c(X_i)\); \(\theta(Y_i)=\pm t^{q}X_i\) of weight \(c(X_i)+q\rho=c(Y_i)\); for \(s\ne i\), \(\theta(Y_s)=\pm(t^{qP_s}X_s-\varepsilon_st^{P_s}Y_s)\) with weights \(c(X_s)+qP_s\rho\ge c(Y_s)\) and \(c(Y_s)\); \(\theta(X_s)=\pm(Y_s-\varepsilon_sX_s)\) with weights \(c(Y_s)-P_s\rho\ge c(X_s)\) and \(c(X_s)\)). Next, computing \(\mathrm{sh}\circ\mathrm{S}\circ g_r\) on each variable with the formulas for \(g_r\) gives
\[
\mathrm{sh}\bigl(\mathrm{S}(g_r\cdot z)\bigr)-\theta(z)=\pm\bigl(\mathcal{E}_{N+[s-i]}\cdot\tilde z-\varepsilon\,\mathcal{E}_{[s-i]}\cdot z\bigr)\quad\text{for }z=Y_s,\qquad =0\ \text{otherwise},
\]
where \(\mathcal{E}_n=(t+\iota_i(r))^{P^{n}}-t^{P^{n}}-\iota_{i+n}(r)\) is the **Frobenius error** (for instance, for \(z=Y_i\): \(g_rY_i=\pm(X_i-\iota_i(r)Y_i)\), \(\mathrm{S}\) gives \(\pm(t^{q}-\iota_i(r))X_i\), and the shift gives \(\pm((t+\iota_i(r))^{q}-\iota_i(r))X_i=\pm(t^{q}+\mathcal{E}_N)X_i\), using \(\iota_{i+N}=\iota_i\)). Each \(\mathcal{E}_n\) is divisible by \(p=w^{2}\): \((t+u)^{P^{n}}\equiv t^{P^{n}}+u^{P^{n}}\pmod p\) and \(\iota_{i+n}(r)\equiv\iota_i(r)^{P^{n}}\pmod p\). As \(\rho\ge0\), every coefficient of \(\mathcal{E}_n\cdot y\) therefore has level at least \(2\) above the weight of the variable \(y\), and one checks \(c(z)+\tfrac1{20}\le c(y)-\mathrm{zero}(y)\rho+2\) for \(y\in\{z,\tilde z\}\) (again from the bounds on increments). So \(\mathrm{sh}(\mathrm{S}(g_rz))-\theta(z)\in G^{0}_{c(z)+1/20}\). Apply the substitution lemma to the polynomial \(g_r^{-1}\cdot f\), which lies in \(\mathrm{G}_b(F^{w},c)\) by (least) since \(g_r^{-1}\in\mathrm{SL}_2^{a,a'}(W)\), with \(\chi=\theta\) and \(\psi=\mathrm{sh}\circ\mathrm{S}\circ g_r\): we get \(\mathrm{sh}(p)=\psi(g_r^{-1}f)\in G^{0}_{b}\). In \(G^{0}\) the variables \(X_i,Y_i\) again have equal weight (\(c(X_i)=c(Y_i)-q\rho\)), so \(t^{j}x^{\bar\alpha}\) has weight \(c\cdot\alpha-(\nu(\alpha)-j)\rho\), and the coefficient of \(t^{j}\) in \(\Phi_\alpha(\mathrm{sh}\,p)=\Phi_\alpha(p)(t+\iota_i(r))\) has order \(\ge l+(\nu(\alpha)-j)\rho>l\) for \(j<\nu(\alpha)\): (iii). \(\square\)
