This chapter covers two folders of the part "Derivatives of theta functions" of the proof: *the integral vacuum* and *the bound is attained*. Its outputs, used by the upper bound for the composite lift and by the comparison of the two sides of the main inequality, are:

* the **integral vacuum** \(\theta^\circ\) and the modules of **standard data** at each prime, with the fact that, for each prime \(p\) and each \(p\)-adic place \(\iota_p\), the derivatives of the theta function of data that are standard at \(\iota_p\), taken at the right scale, are integral at \(\iota_p\), and have the predicted divisibility there if \(p\) does not split (standard data at \(p\) form a module over the numbers integral at \(\iota_p\), so nothing is asserted at other primes: integrality of one datum at all primes needs standardness at every place);
* the **sharpness theorem**: the predicted divisibility \(S_p\) cannot be improved, uniformly in \(p\), up to one number \(t\) of controlled size.

## Notation

Throughout, \(P\) are the [[?lean:New.Parameters|parameters]] (a modulus \(q_0\) and a prime \(d\equiv7\bmod8\)), \(K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})\), and \(E=K(\zeta)\) with \(\zeta\) a primitive \(q_1\)-th root of unity; \((\Phi_\tau)\) is the CM type, indexed by the infinite places of \(E\), which form a group \(\Phi\cong\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/q_1)^\times\). The lattice \(\mathfrak{a}_0=E\cap\alpha\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_E\) is defined by the [[?lean:New.Parameters.α|idele \(\alpha\)]] of the Weil data; \(\mathfrak{a}_0\overline{\mathfrak{a}_0}\) is the inverse different of \(E/K\). For a prime \(p\), \(\iota_p\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}_p\) is a [[?lean:New.PadicPlace|\(p\)-adic place]], and "integral at \(p\)" means \(\lvert\iota_p(\cdot)\rvert_p\le1\). For a hermitian space \(V\) over \(E\) and \(f\) in its Schwartz space, \(J_a(f)\) is the [[?lean:New.Space.jetSeries|jet series]] at scale \(a\): for standard \(V\) and \(f\) in the finite part it is the Taylor series at \(0\) of \(z\mapsto\Theta_f(az)\), \(\Theta_f\) the entire theta function. From the second half on, \(D\) is [[?lean:New.Data|admissible data]] with scales \(x,x_1,b\), ranks \(m,r,k\), level \(M=p_0p_1\), weights \(\kappa(\tau)\) and \(b\)-places \(\Phi_b\).

## 1. The scale and theta functions from tight frames

The CM curve \(\mathcal{E}=\mathbb{C}/\Omega_0\mathcal{O}_K\) and its period are fixed in [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/cm-curve-and-period|the CM curve and the period]]; the Chowla–Selberg formula supplies [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/chowla-selberg-unit|a unit \(u\)]]. With the vacuum root \(s_0\) these define the period factor \(\varpi=s_0/u\) and [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/jet-scale|the jet scale]] \(\lambda=\varpi\sqrt{2\pi/\sqrt d}/\Omega_0\). Arithmetic of the scale: [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/hermitian-product-of-lattice-vectors-is-integral|hermitian products of vectors of \(\mathfrak{a}_0\) are integers of \(K\)]]; [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/p-adic-size-of-vacuum-root|the \(p\)-adic size of \(s_0\)]] and [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/p-adic-size-of-period-factor|of \(\varpi\)]] are those of \(\alpha^{-1}\), so that [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/scaled-linear-forms-are-p-integral|the scaled linear forms \(\varpi\,y^\dagger\) of vectors integral above \(p\) are \(p\)-integral]]; and [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/modulus-of-jet-scale|\(\lvert\lambda\rvert^2=\Omega^{-1}\)]].

A *tight frame* of length \(M\) is a family of vectors of \(\mathfrak{a}_0\) with multiplicities whose Gram matrix over the CM type is \(M\) times the identity. [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/roots-of-unity-tight-frame|The powers of \(\zeta\) are a tight frame of length \(q_1\)]]. On the analytic side, [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/taylor-series-on-a-line-from-jet-series|the jet series restricts to the Taylor series on every line]], hence [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/vector-determined-by-jet-series|determines the vector]], and [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/jet-series-of-product-of-linear-forms|a theta function that is a product of functions of linear forms has the product of their Taylor series as jet series]]; see also [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/jets-along-homogeneous-polynomials|jets along homogeneous polynomials]].

The central construction is the [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/resolution-product|resolution product]]: a vector whose rational translates have theta functions equal to products of translated sections of a CM lattice along the frame. Because [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/frame-forms-sum-to-standard-hermitian-form|the frame forms sum to the standard hermitian form]], Fourier inversion gives [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/existence-of-resolution-product-along-tight-frame|the existence theorem]]: a product of monomial sections \(\theta^r(\sigma^2)^tG^u\) along a tight frame is the theta function of a vector, with [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/resolution-product-theta-values-and-jets|explicit theta values and jets]] and [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/resolution-product-integral-values|integral values]] (they are products of torsion values).

## 2. The integral vacuum and standard data

Taking the frame of powers of \(\zeta\) and the section \(\theta\) gives [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/theta-product|theta products]], which [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/existence-of-theta-products|exist]]. By Fourier inversion [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/vacuum-as-sum-of-translates|a multiple of the vacuum is a sum of translates of a theta product]], whence [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/integral-vacuum-in-rank-one|the integral vacuum in rank one]]. This defines [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/integral-vacuum|the integral vacuum \(\theta^\circ\)]] and [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/standard-data|standard data at \(p\)]]. [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/integral-vacuum-values|The values of \(\theta^\circ\) are integral, bounded at all conjugates and not all zero]], using [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/norm-bounded-by-values-at-finite-points|a bound of the norm by values at finite points]]. Complex multiplication yields [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/conjugates-of-jets-of-translates|the conjugates of the jets of the translates of a generating vector]]. Standard data are [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/stability-of-standard-data|stable under integral Heisenberg and Weil operators]].

## 3. Tight frames in the lattice

To work at primes dividing \(q_1\) and at the level \(M\) one needs frames of other lengths. [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/tight-frame-operations|Frames can be united and pairs replaced by sums]]; [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/lattice-a0-is-galois-stable|\(\mathfrak{a}_0\) is Galois stable with integral norms]], so [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/orbit-tight-frame|orbits are tight frames]]. With [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/dual-vectors|the dual vectors]] and [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/dual-vectors-properties|their properties]] one gets [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/tight-frame-of-length-prime-to-q1|a tight frame of length prime to \(q_1\)]]. A vector with a [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/unit-linear-form|unit linear form]] [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/existence-of-vector-with-unit-linear-form|exists at every prime]]. By the coin problem there are [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/tight-frames-of-all-large-lengths|frames of all large lengths]]; for admissible data [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/short-coprime-tight-frame|the coprime frame is short]], and there is [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/tight-frame-of-length-m|a tight frame of length \(M\) with multiplicities \(\ge6fb\)]].

## 4. Integrality and divisibility of the derivatives

On one curve, [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/jets-integral-in-derivative-stable-ring|jets are integral on a derivative-stable ring]]; [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/jet-ring|the jet ring]] [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/jet-ring-is-stable-under-derivatives|is such a ring]] when the forms are \(d\) times integral forms, which gives [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/integrality-of-jets-of-theta-products|integrality of jets of theta products]]. To apply this to standard data: [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/resolution-from-tight-frame|a tight frame of square length gives a resolution of the identity]], [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/tight-frame-of-square-length-prime-to-p|such a frame exists with length prime to \(p\)]], [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/product-of-translated-thetas-as-theta-power|products of thetas translated by division points are theta powers times integral series]], and the integral vacuum has [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/vacuum-expansion|a vacuum expansion]] ([[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/existence-of-vacuum-expansion|existence]]). Hence [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/standard-data-as-theta-products|standard data are theta products along a resolution times integral series]], and therefore:

* [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/integrality-of-derivatives-of-standard-data|integrality at \(\iota_p\) of the derivatives of data standard at \(\iota_p\)]], for every prime \(p\), split or not;
* [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/normal-form-of-standard-data|the normal form at non-split primes]], and with it [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/divisibility-of-derivatives-along-minors|the divisibility of derivatives along products of minors and Schur entries]].

## 5. Sharpness on one curve and least valuations

The rate \(\gamma_p=1/(p-1)-\rho_p\) is optimal on one curve: with [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/largest-coefficient-among-integral-series|the lemma on largest coefficients]], [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/greatest-norm-of-derivatives|the greatest norm of derivatives]] [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/greatest-norm-of-derivatives-is-submultiplicative|is submultiplicative]]; [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/derivatives-of-the-formal-parameter|the formal parameter]] attains the rate at powers of \(q_p\); by [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/submultiplicative-sequences|a Fekete-type lemma]] this gives [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/katz-divisibility-limit|the limit of Katz divisibility]].

For several variables one studies [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/least-valuation-relation|the least valuation \(\operatorname{val}_n(\nu)\)]] of coefficients of products over \(n\) copies, with [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/least-valuation-rules|its rules]], using [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/copy-substitution|copy substitutions]] and [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/lowest-term-of-a-power-series|lowest terms]]. The key device: [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/six-scaled-copies-are-a-unit|six scaled copies of a theta product multiply to a unit]], so the factor \(\Theta\) can be removed at the cost of six copies. Consequences: [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/least-valuation-with-a-unit-form|the bound with a unit form]], [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/least-valuation-zero-when-forms-span|least valuation at most zero when the forms span]], and [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/integral-series-in-parameters-after-scaling|integrality after scaling]].

At inert primes the gain comes from couplings: for a [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/coupling-family|coupling family]], [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/derivatives-of-exponential-factorizations|differentiating \(W\exp(\kappa\Xi)\)]] and [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/unit-coefficient-at-the-power-degree|a unit coefficient at the power degree]] give [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/coefficient-of-a-product-of-couplings|the exact valuation of the coefficient of a product of couplings]], hence [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/least-valuation-along-couplings|least valuations along couplings]].

## 6. Near pairs and the valuation bound at every prime

Which couplings are available is governed by the \(b\)-places: by [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/sparseness-along-powers-of-minus-p|sparseness]], [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/near-pair|near pairs]] [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/near-pairs-are-disjoint|are disjoint]]. The losses are absorbed by [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/loss-product|the loss product]]: [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/legendre-bounds-for-the-data|Legendre bounds]], [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/partner-minimum-sum|the partner minimum sum \(R_\tau\)]], [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/rate-minus-predicted-valuation|the identity \(\gamma_p\lvert\kappa\rvert-S_p=v(\pi_p)\sum R_\tau\)]], [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/bound-for-the-partner-minimum-sum|the bound for \(R_\tau\)]] and [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/errors-covered-by-the-loss-product|the errors covered by \(v(B)\)]].

For a tight frame of length \(M\) one uses [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/resolution-forms-of-a-frame|its resolution forms]] and [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/properties-of-resolution-forms|their properties]], [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/least-valuation-zero-at-inert-primes|least valuation at most zero at inert primes]] and [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/normal-form-along-a-tight-frame|the normal form along the frame]]. For [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/degree-near-the-weights|degrees near the weights]] the three cases [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/valuation-bound-at-split-primes|split]], [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/valuation-bound-at-large-inert-primes|inert above \(k\)]] and [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/valuation-bound-at-small-non-split-primes|non-split at most \(k\)]] give [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/valuation-bound-on-twelve-copies|the valuation bound on twelve copies]]:
\[ v_p(l!)+\operatorname{val}_{12}(l)\le S_p+2\sum_\tau v_p(B(l_\tau)). \]

## 7. The bound is attained

On [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/twelve-space-and-indicator-data|the space of twelve copies]], [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/theta-product-times-monomials-as-a-vector|theta products times monomials in \(y\) are Taylor series of integral vectors]], and [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/expansion-in-indicator-data|such vectors are bounded by indicator data]]; this is [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/reduction-to-the-curve|the reduction to the curve]]. With [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/size-of-the-sharpness-constant|the size of the constant]] one obtains the main result of the second half, [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/sharpness-of-the-valuation-bound|the sharpness of the valuation bound]]: for every degree \(l\) between \(\kappa\) and \(\kappa+x^{42}\) there is one \(t\ne0\) with all conjugates at most \(e^{fmb/k}\) such that at every prime some Taylor coefficient of degree \(l\) of an indicator datum modulo \(\pi_M\), times \(c_{\mathrm{vac}}^{12}\prod l_\tau!/t\), has valuation at most \(S_p\).

## Status

Results proved in other areas and only cited here: the Fock-model integral formulas and finite Fourier inversion for theta functions (Lib, Weil construction), complex multiplication and the Chowla–Selberg formula for the CM curve (Lib, CM), the integrality principles of the formal group of the curve and the normal form of theta products at non-split primes (formal-group chapter), the count of divisibility for series in normal form (chapter on derivatives that count primes), and the scale inequalities of the data (verified in Lean by its magnitude calculus). Each knowl states which of these it uses.
