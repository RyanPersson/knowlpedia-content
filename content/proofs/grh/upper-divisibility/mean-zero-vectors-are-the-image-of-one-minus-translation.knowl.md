**Lemma.** Let \(G\) be a commutative [[algebra-groups/group|group]], \(\pi\) a [[?c:Linear representation of a group|representation]] of \(G\) on a complex vector space \(V\), \((z_w)_{w\in I}\) a finite family of elements of \(G\), and \(r\ge1\) an integer with \(\pi(z_w)^{r}=1\) for all \(w\). Then
\[
\bigcap_{w\in I}\ker\Bigl(\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\pi(z_w)^{i}\Bigr)\;=\;\operatorname{im}\ \pi\Bigl(\prod_{w\in I}(1-z_w)\Bigr),
\]
where the product is taken in the [[algebra-representation-theory/group-algebra|group algebra]] \(\mathbb{C}[G]\). In particular (one operator): if \(T\) is an endomorphism of \(V\) with \(T^{r}=1\), then \(\sum_{i<r}T^{i}\varphi=0\) if and only if \(\varphi=\psi-T\psi\) for some \(\psi\in V\).

## Proof

Work in the commutative ring \(\mathbb{C}[G]\) and put \(\sigma_w=\sum_{i<r}z_w^{i}\).

(\(\subseteq\)) Since \(1-z_w\) divides \(1-z_w^{i}\) for each \(i\), it divides \(r-\sigma_w=\sum_{i<r}(1-z_w^{i})\). Hence \(\prod_w(1-z_w)\) divides \(\prod_w(r-\sigma_w)\), say \(\prod_w(r-\sigma_w)=q\prod_w(1-z_w)\). If \(\pi(\sigma_w)\varphi=0\) for all \(w\), then \(\pi(r-\sigma_w)\varphi=r\varphi\), and by induction on the factors (they commute, and each maps \(\varphi\) to a multiple of \(\varphi\)) \(\pi\bigl(\prod_w(r-\sigma_w)\bigr)\varphi=r^{\lvert I\rvert}\varphi\). Therefore \(r^{\lvert I\rvert}\varphi=\pi\bigl(\prod_w(1-z_w)\bigr)\pi(q)\varphi\) lies in the image, and so does \(\varphi\) because \(r\ne0\) in \(\mathbb{C}\).

(\(\supseteq\)) For each \(w\), \(\sigma_w(1-z_w)=1-z_w^{r}\) acts as \(0\). As \(\prod_{u}(1-z_u)=(1-z_w)\prod_{u\ne w}(1-z_u)\), the operator \(\pi(\sigma_w)\) kills the image of \(\pi\bigl(\prod_u(1-z_u)\bigr)\).

The statement for one operator is the case of the cyclic group generated by \(T\); directly, \(r\varphi=(1-T)S\varphi\) with \(\sum_{i<r}(1-T^{i})=(1-T)S\), so \(\psi=r^{-1}S\varphi\). \(\blacksquare\)
