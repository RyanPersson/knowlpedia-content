**Theorem.** Let \(D\) be [[?lean:New.Data|admissible data]], \(p\) an [[proofs/grh/upper-divisibility/classes-of-primes|exceptional]] prime, \(\iota\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}_p\) an embedding, \(w_\tau\) the [[proofs/grh/upper-divisibility/marked-set-of-primes|prime induced by]] \(\tau\in\Phi\), \(g=u(-p)\in\Phi\cong(\mathbb{Z}/q_1)^\times\) the place named by \(-p\), \(o\) its order, \(o_0\) the order of \(-p\) modulo \(q_0\), \(\mathfrak r\) the least number with \(m\le p^{\mathfrak r}\), and \(l_0\) the least power of \(3\) above \(27s\), where \(2^{s}=m^{22}\). Then:

1. \(o_0\) is odd and \(o_0\le\mathfrak r\le l_0\); also \(\mathfrak r\le s\).
2. \(m\mid o\).
3. Every prime of \(E\) above \(p\) has absolute norm \(p^{o}\); in particular each \(w_\tau\).
4. For odd \(e\) and every \(\tau\): \(w_{u(-p^{e})\tau}=\overline{w_\tau}\).
5. \(\overline{w}\ne w\) for every prime \(w\) of \(E\) above \(p\).
6. For \(\sigma,\tau\in\Phi\): \(w_\sigma\in\{w_\tau,\overline{w_\tau}\}\) if and only if \(\sigma=g^{i}\tau\) for some \(i\ge0\).

Moreover, for any prime \(p<x_1\) with \(\chi_K(p)\ne1\): \(p\nmid M\), \(p\ne N\), \(p\nmid MN\).

## Proof

(1) \(o_0\) odd and \(o_0\le\mathfrak r\) are part of the definition. As \(p\ge2\) and \(m=2^{e+1}\) with \(s=22(e+1)\), \(m\le2^{s}\le p^{s}\), so \(\mathfrak r\le s\); and \(m\le m^{594}\le2^{l_0}\le p^{l_0}\) ([[?lean:New.Data.m_pow_le_two_pow_l₀|`m_pow_le_two_pow_l₀`]], quoted) gives \(\mathfrak r\le l_0\).

(2) \(p\) is inert in \(K\), so \(p\nmid q_1d\) and \(-p\) is a unit modulo \(q_1\); \(2^{s+1}\mid q_1\). Hence \((-p)^{o}\equiv1\pmod{2^{s+1}}\). By lifting the exponent at \(2\) ([[?lean:New.two_pow_dvd_or_of_pow_eq_one|`two_pow_dvd_or_of_pow_eq_one`]], quoted) \(2^{s}\) divides \((-p-1)\,o\) or \((-p+1)\,o\), that is \((p+1)o\) or \((p-1)o\). Since \(p\not\equiv\pm1\bmod m\), \(2^{e+1}\) divides neither \(p-1\) nor \(p+1\); the \(2\)-adic valuation of the first factor is at most \(e\) and \(s>2e\), so \(v_2(o)\ge s-e\ge e+1\): \(m\mid o\).

(3) For \(p\) inert in \(K\), a prime of \(E=K(\zeta_{q_1})\) above \(p\) has norm \(p^{\operatorname{lcm}(o,2)}\) ([[?lean:New.Parameters.q_eq_pow_lcm_of_inert|`q_eq_pow_lcm_of_inert`]], quoted), and \(2\mid m\mid o\).

(4)–(6) The group \(\Phi=\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)\) acts on the primes above \(p\), and \(w_{\sigma\tau}=\tau\cdot w_\sigma\) (the pullback of \(w_\sigma\) along \(\tau\)). For \(p\) inert in \(K\) the source proves ([[?lean:New.Parameters.smul_eq_self_iff_of_inert|`smul_eq_self_iff_of_inert`]], [[?lean:New.Parameters.smul_eq_star_iff_of_inert|`smul_eq_star_iff_of_inert`]], quoted): \(\rho\cdot w=w\) iff \(\rho\) is an even power of \(g\), and \(\rho\cdot w=\bar w\) iff \(\rho\) is an odd power of \(g\). (The decomposition group of \(w\) in \(\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q})\) is generated by the Frobenius, which is complex conjugation times \(g\) up to sign conventions; its intersection with \(\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)\) is generated by \(g^2\).) This gives (4) with \(u(-p^{e})=g^{e}\) for odd \(e\), and (6) by writing \(w_\sigma=(\sigma/\tau)\cdot w_\tau\). For (5): if \(\bar w=w\) then the identity is an odd power \(g^{i}\) of \(g\), so \(o\mid i\) with \(i\) odd, contradicting \(2\mid o\).

The last assertion: the prime factors \(p_0,p_1\) of \(M\) and \(N\) are at least \(x_1>p\) (size comparisons, quoted). \(\blacksquare\)

*Status.* The lifting-the-exponent lemma, the norm of primes above an inert \(p\), and the description of the stabiliser of a prime above \(p\) are quoted from other files.
