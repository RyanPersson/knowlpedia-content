Notation as in [[proofs/grh/derivatives-vacuum/hermitian-product-of-lattice-vectors-is-integral|the lemma on hermitian products]]. Let \(p\) be a prime and \(\iota_p\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}_p\) a [[?lean:New.PadicPlace|\(p\)-adic place]] of \(\mathbb{C}\), with valuation ring \(R=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\lvert\iota_p z\rvert_p\le1\}\). For an infinite place \(\tau\) let \(\varphi_\tau\colon E\to\mathbb{C}_p\), \(y\mapsto\iota_p(\Phi_\tau(\bar y))\) (the [[?lean:New.weightEmbedding|weight embedding]]), and let \(\widehat\varphi_\tau\colon\mathbb{A}_{E,f}\to\mathbb{C}_p\) be its [[?lean:New.adeleExtension|extension to the finite adeles]]: it takes the component at the prime of \(E\) induced by \(\varphi_\tau\) and applies the continuous extension of \(\varphi_\tau\) to that completion. Let \(s_0\) be the [[?lean:New.Parameters.vacuumRoot|vacuum root]].

**Lemma.** For every infinite place \(\tau\) of \(E\):

1. \(\lvert\iota_p(s_0)\rvert_p=\lvert\widehat\varphi_\tau(\alpha)\rvert_p^{-1}\);
2. the \(R\)-submodule of \(\mathbb{C}\) generated by \(s_0\) equals the \(R\)-submodule generated by \(\{\overline{\Phi_\tau(y)}: y\in E,\ \alpha y\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_E\}\), that is, by \(\overline{\Phi_\tau(\mathfrak{a}_0^{-1})}\).

## Proof

Let \(g=P'(\zeta)\) generate the relative different and \(\beta=(1+\sqrt{-d})/2\in K\subset E\). By [[?lean:New.Parameters.α|definition]], the component of \(\alpha\) at a finite place \(w\) is \(g^{-1}\) if \(\beta\in w\) and \(1\) otherwise. Hence for any embedding \(\varphi\colon E\to\mathbb{C}_p\), inducing the prime \(w_\varphi\),
\[ \widehat\varphi(\alpha)=\begin{cases}\varphi(g)^{-1}&\beta\in w_\varphi,\\ 1&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}\tag{*} \]

*Independence of \(\tau\).* The condition \(\beta\in w_{\varphi_\tau}\) reads \(\lvert\iota_p(\overline{\Phi_\tau(\beta)})\rvert_p<1\), and \(\Phi_\tau(\beta)=(1+i\sqrt d)/2\) for all \(\tau\) because \(\beta\in K\); so the condition does not depend on \(\tau\). Moreover \(\Phi_\tau(g)=\Phi_1(\gamma_\tau g)\) for the element \(\gamma_\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)\) corresponding to \(\tau\), and \(\gamma_\tau g\) and \(g\) generate the same ideal, the different being Galois-stable ([[?lean:New.Parameters.valuation_map_relDifferentGen|all conjugates of \(g\) over \(K\) have the same valuation at every place]]). Therefore \(\lvert\varphi_\tau(g)\rvert_p=\lvert\varphi_1(g)\rvert_p\), and by \((*)\), \(\lvert\widehat\varphi_u(\alpha)\rvert_p=\lvert\widehat\varphi_\tau(\alpha)\rvert_p\) for all infinite places \(u\).

*Part 1.* By [[?lean:New.WeilData.norm_map_chiFin|the absolute value of \(\chi_{\mathrm{fin}}\) at a \(p\)-adic place]], \(\lvert\iota_p\chi_{\mathrm{fin}}(\alpha)\rvert_p=\prod_u\lvert\widehat\varphi_u(\alpha)\rvert_p=\lvert\widehat\varphi_\tau(\alpha)\rvert_p^{f}\), where \(f\) is the number of infinite places. Since \(s_0^{f}=\chi_{\mathrm{fin}}(\alpha)^{-1}\), we get \(\lvert\iota_p s_0\rvert_p^{f}=\lvert\widehat\varphi_\tau(\alpha)\rvert_p^{-f}\), and taking \(f\)-th roots of nonnegative reals gives the claim.

*Part 2.* By part 1, \(s_0\ne0\) and for every \(y\in E\)
\[ \bigl\lvert\iota_p\bigl(\overline{\Phi_\tau(y)}/s_0\bigr)\bigr\rvert_p=\lvert\varphi_\tau(y)\rvert_p\,\lvert\widehat\varphi_\tau(\alpha)\rvert_p=\lvert\widehat\varphi_\tau(\alpha y)\rvert_p .\]
If \(\alpha y\) is integral the right side is at most \(1\), so \(\overline{\Phi_\tau(y)}\in R\,s_0\); this is the inclusion \(\supseteq\). For \(\subseteq\) it suffices to find \(y\in E\) with \(\alpha y\) integral and \(\lvert\widehat\varphi_\tau(\alpha y)\rvert_p=1\), for then \(s_0/\overline{\Phi_\tau(y)}\in R\). Since \(E\) is dense in the finite adeles ([[?c:Strong approximation theorem|strong approximation]]) and \(\{x: p^{-1}(\alpha x-1)\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_E\}\) is open and nonempty (it contains \(\alpha^{-1}\)), there is \(y\in E\) with \(\alpha y=1+p\,c\), \(c\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_E\). Then \(\alpha y\) is integral, and \(\lvert\widehat\varphi_\tau(pc)\rvert_p\le\lvert p\rvert_p<1\) gives \(\lvert\widehat\varphi_\tau(\alpha y)\rvert_p=1\) by the ultrametric inequality.
