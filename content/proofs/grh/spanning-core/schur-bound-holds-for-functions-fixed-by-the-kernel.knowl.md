**Theorem (Schur's bound).** Fix a unitary sign frame for signs \(\varepsilon\) on \(G_1\) (rank \(r\), \(n=\varrho+1\)), a doubled parameter \(l\) with [[?lean:New.HCParam.HasLargeGap|large gap]] and entries of modulus \(\le L\), coefficient \(c_\lambda\), formal degree \(\deg_\lambda\), and \(e=\deg_\lambda c_\lambda\). Let \(F\colon G_1\to\mathbb{C}^I\) be a [[?lean:New.IsBesselFunction|Bessel function of type \(\mathrm{rep}\)]], integrable for the measure \(\mu\) of \(\mathsf U\backslash G_1\), with \(F*e=F\), where \((F*k)(y)=\int k(a^{-1})F(ya)\,da\). Let \(B\ge(r+1)^4(L+r+1+2n^2)\). Then \(F\) [[proofs/grh/spanning-core/schur-bound-for-a-bessel-function|has the Schur bound]] with constants \(B\) and \(D=\deg_\lambda^{-1}\int\lVert F\rVert^2d\mu\).

**Derivative kernels.** There are integrable functions \(X_w\) on \(G_1\), indexed by words \(w\) in pairs of basis indices, with \(X_\emptyset=e\), \(e*X_w=X_w\), \(\deg_\lambda^{-1}\int\lvert X_w\rvert^2\le\bigl((r+1)^4(L+r+1+\lvert w\rvert)\bigr)^{2\lvert w\rvert}\), and such that for every bounded measurable \(F\) with \(F*e=F\) the functions \(F*X_w\) are the iterated right derivatives of \(F\): along a curve with \(\gamma'(t)=\gamma(t)Z\), \((F*X_w)\circ\gamma\) has derivative \(\sum_{a,b}Z_{ab}(F*X_{(a,b)w})(\gamma(t))\).

**Matrix coefficient.** For \(F\) as in the theorem, \(\int\langle F(y),F(yg)\rangle\,d\mu(y)=c_\lambda(g)\int\lVert F\rVert^2d\mu\), hence \(\int_{G_1}\bigl\lvert\int\langle F(y),F(yg)\rangle d\mu(y)\bigr\rvert^2dg=\deg_\lambda^{-1}\bigl(\int\lVert F\rVert^2d\mu\bigr)^2\).

## Proof

*Outline.* The inputs are from the library on discrete series (statements read at the call sites, not reproved): \(e\) is a continuous, bounded, integrable idempotent kernel (\(e*e=e\)); there is a smooth compactly supported bump \(\beta\) on \(U(\varepsilon)\) with \(c_\lambda*\beta=c_\lambda\) (`New.exists_smear_dsCoeffSmooth_eq`); the \(U(\mathfrak g)\)-module generated by \(c_\lambda\) carries a filtration by degree and the norm \(\lVert x\rVert^2=\deg_\lambda\int\lvert x\rvert^2\), and a matrix \(M\) maps step \(s\) to step \(s+1\) with \(\lVert Mx\rVert\le(r+1)^4(L+r+1+s)\lVert M\rVert\lVert x\rVert\) (`IsKHighest.norm_apply_le_of_mem_filtration`); and the abstract Schur inequality for convolutions `New.IsBesselFunction.lintegral_sq_convolve_convolve_le` and the coefficient identity `New.IsBesselFunction.integral_inner_translate`.

*Kernels.* Let \(\beta_w\) be the derivative of \(\beta\) along the word \(w\) (matrix units conjugated by the frame, which have norm \(1\)), and \(X_w=e*\beta_w\) (pulled back to \(G_1\) by the frame, which preserves Haar measure). Then \(X_\emptyset=e\), \(X_w\) is integrable, \(e*X_w=(e*e)*\beta_w=X_w\). \(X_w/\deg_\lambda=c_\lambda*\beta_w\) is the vector obtained from \(c_\lambda\) by applying \(\lvert w\rvert\) matrices of norm \(1\), so by induction on the word its norm is at most \(((r+1)^4(L+r+1+\lvert w\rvert))^{\lvert w\rvert}\) (the generator has norm \(\le1\)); this is the bound for \(\deg_\lambda^{-1}\int\lvert X_w\rvert^2\). For \(F=F*e\): \(F*X_w=F*\beta_w\), and differentiating the convolution with a bump along a curve differentiates the bump; expanding \(Z\) in matrix units gives the chain rule.

*Theorem.* Put \(V_w=F*X_w\) (continuous; a Bessel function, since left translations commute with right convolution). Part (b) of the Schur bound is the chain rule. For (c), with \(\lvert w\rvert\le2n^2\) and \(\varphi\) integrable, \(g\mapsto\int\varphi(y)V_w(gy)\,dy=(V_w*\check\varphi)(g)\), and the abstract Schur inequality gives
\[
\int\lVert(F*X_w)*\check\varphi\rVert^2d\mu\le\frac{\int\lvert X_w\rvert^2}{\deg_\lambda}\cdot\frac{\int\lVert F\rVert^2d\mu}{\deg_\lambda}\cdot\int\lvert\varphi\rvert^2\le B^{2\lvert w\rvert}D\int\lvert\varphi\rvert^2 .
\]

*Matrix coefficient.* The coefficient identity (library) gives the first formula; the second follows from \(\int_{G_1}\lvert c_\lambda\rvert^2=\deg_\lambda^{-1}\), the definition of the formal degree. \(\blacksquare\)
