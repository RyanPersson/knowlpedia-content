The **quaternion division algebra** is the four-dimensional real [[linear-algebra/vector-space|vector space]]
\[
\mathbb H
=
\{a+bi+cj+dk\mid a,b,c,d\in\mathbb R\}
\]
with bilinear multiplication determined by
\[
i^2=j^2=k^2=ijk=-1.
\]
Equivalently, \(ij=k=-ji\), \(jk=i=-kj\), and \(ki=j=-ik\). With this multiplication, \(\mathbb H\) is an associative, noncommutative [[algebra-rings/division-ring|division ring]] whose center is \(\mathbb R\). It is also a unital algebra over the [[shared-foundations/real-numbers|real numbers]]. The standard embedding [[shared-foundations/complex-numbers-c|\(\mathbb C\)]] \(\hookrightarrow\mathbb H\) sends the complex imaginary unit to \(i\).

## Conjugation, norm, and inverse

For \(q=a+bi+cj+dk\), define
\[
\overline q=a-bi-cj-dk.
\]
Quaternionic conjugation reverses products:
\[
\overline{pq}=\overline q\,\overline p.
\]
The [[linear-algebra/norm|norm]] is
\[
|q|
=
\sqrt{q\overline q}
=
\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2}.
\]
It is multiplicative, so \(|pq|=|p||q|\). Every nonzero quaternion therefore has the two-sided inverse
\[
q^{-1}=\frac{\overline q}{|q|^2},
\]
which proves directly that \(\mathbb H\) is a division algebra.

## Scalar and vector parts

Write \(q=a+\mathbf v\), where \(a\in\mathbb R\) and \(\mathbf v\in\mathbb R^3\) is identified with \(bi+cj+dk\). Then
\[
(a+\mathbf v)(b+\mathbf w)
=
(ab-\mathbf v\mathbin{\cdot}\mathbf w)
+(a\mathbf w+b\mathbf v+\mathbf v\times\mathbf w).
\]
Thus the failure of commutativity records the cross product: the commutator of two purely imaginary quaternions is twice their vector cross product.

## Unit quaternions

The elements of norm one form a group isomorphic to \(\operatorname{Sp}(1)\) and \(\operatorname{SU}(2)\). Conjugation \(v\mapsto qvq^{-1}\) by a unit quaternion preserves the three-dimensional space of imaginary quaternions and its [[linear-algebra/euclidean-norm|Euclidean norm]]. This gives the double covering \(\operatorname{Sp}(1)\to\operatorname{SO}(3)\), with kernel \(\{\pm1\}\).

## Remarks

The algebra \(\mathbb H\) is not the quaternion group \(Q_8\). The latter is the finite multiplicative subgroup \(\{\pm1,\pm i,\pm j,\pm k\}\) inside \(\mathbb H^\times\). Quaternionic vector spaces also require a choice of left or right scalar multiplication because \(\mathbb H\) is noncommutative.
