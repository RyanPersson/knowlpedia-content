Let \(G\) be a [[topology/locally-compact-group|locally compact group]] and let \(\lambda\) be its left
[[harmonic-analysis/regular-representations-locally-compact-group|regular
representation]] on \(L^2(G)\). The **Fourier algebra** \(A(G)\) consists of
the [[harmonic-analysis/coefficient-function|coefficient functions]]
\[
u(x)=\langle\lambda(x)\xi,\eta\rangle,
\qquad \xi,\eta\in L^2(G).
\]
Its norm is
\[
\|u\|_{A(G)}
=\inf\{\|\xi\|_2\|\eta\|_2:
u(x)=\langle\lambda(x)\xi,\eta\rangle\}.
\]
With pointwise multiplication and this norm, \(A(G)\) is a commutative Banach
algebra of continuous functions vanishing at infinity. The norm is intrinsic,
even though its definition uses coefficient factorizations.

## Operator-algebraic interpretation

Let \(VN(G)\) be the [[operator-algebras/von-neumann-algebra|von Neumann algebra]] generated by
\(\lambda(G)\). Each coefficient \(u=u_{\xi,\eta}\) defines the normal
functional
\[
T\longmapsto\langle T\xi,\eta\rangle
\]
on \(VN(G)\), and this identifies \(A(G)\) isometrically with the canonical
predual \(VN(G)_*\). Under this identification, pointwise multiplication in
\(A(G)\) is dual to the comultiplication
\(\lambda(x)\mapsto\lambda(x)\otimes\lambda(x)\)
[Eymard, §§2–3].

## Abelian case

If \(G\) is locally compact abelian with
[[harmonic-analysis/pontryagin-dual|Pontryagin dual]] \(\widehat G\), then
\[
A(G)=\{\widehat f:f\in L^1(\widehat G)\},
\qquad
\|\widehat f\|_{A(G)}=\|f\|_{L^1(\widehat G)}.
\]
Thus the Fourier algebra is the transform of the \(L^1\)-algebra on the
**dual** group. Except under a chosen self-duality, it should not be confused
with \(L^1(G)\) itself.

## Relation to the Fourier–Stieltjes algebra

The [[harmonic-analysis/fourier-stieltjes-algebra|Fourier–Stieltjes algebra]]
\(B(G)\) uses coefficient functions of all
continuous unitary representations of \(G\), whereas \(A(G)\) uses only the
left [[algebra-representation-theory/regular-representation|regular representation]]. Hence \(A(G)\subseteq B(G)\). The smaller
algebra is a closed ideal in \(B(G)\) and is tailored to the regular
representation and the [[operator-algebras/group-von-neumann-algebra|group von Neumann algebra]], rather than to the full
group \(C^*\)-algebra.

## Examples

For a discrete group, functions in \(A(G)\) still vanish at infinity, but
\(A(G)\) is generally not \(\ell^1(G)\). For a compact group, Peter–Weyl
theory describes \(A(G)\) through matrix coefficients of irreducible
representations with a weighted trace-class norm. These examples show that
the \(A(G)\)-norm is representation-theoretic rather than a pointwise or
[[real-analysis/supremum-norm|supremum norm]].
