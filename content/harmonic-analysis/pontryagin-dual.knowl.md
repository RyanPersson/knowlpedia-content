Let \(G\) be a [[topology/locally-compact-group|locally compact Hausdorff group]] that is [[algebra-groups/abelian-group|abelian]]. Inside [[shared-foundations/complex-numbers-c|the complex numbers]], let
\[
\mathbb T=\{z\in\mathbb C:\lvert z\rvert=1\}
\]
be the circle group. The **Pontryagin dual** of \(G\) is
\[
\widehat G=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(G,\mathbb T),
\]
the set of continuous [[algebra-groups/group-homomorphism|group homomorphisms]] \(\chi:G\to\mathbb T\), called characters. Pointwise multiplication, \((\chi\psi)(x)=\chi(x)\psi(x)\), makes \(\widehat G\) an abelian group. It carries the compact-open topology, generated by uniform control of characters on compact subsets of \(G\); with this topology, \(\widehat G\) is again locally compact and Hausdorff.

## Biduality

Each \(x\in G\) defines a character on \(\widehat G\) by evaluation, \(\chi\mapsto\chi(x)\). The [[harmonic-analysis/pontryagin-duality-theorem|Pontryagin duality theorem]] says that
\[
G\longrightarrow\widehat{\widehat G},\qquad x\longmapsto(\chi\mapsto\chi(x)),
\]
is an isomorphism of [[topology/topological-group|topological groups]]. The compact-open topology is essential: the statement is stronger than an abstract [[algebra-groups/group-isomorphism|group isomorphism]] [Rudin, Chapter 1](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781118165621).

## Basic examples

With the usual normalizations,
\[
\widehat{\mathbb R}\cong\mathbb R,\qquad
t\longmapsto(x\longmapsto e^{2\pi itx}),
\]
while \(\widehat{\mathbb Z}\cong\mathbb T\) and \(\widehat{\mathbb T}\cong\mathbb Z\). More generally, the dual of a compact abelian group is discrete, and the dual of a discrete abelian group is compact. Finite abelian groups are isomorphic to their duals, though generally not by a canonical isomorphism.

## Fourier pairing and conventions

The evaluation \(\langle x,\chi\rangle=\chi(x)\) is the canonical pairing between \(G\) and \(\widehat G\). After choosing a [[harmonic-analysis/haar-measure|Haar measure]], it defines the Fourier transform
\[
\widehat f(\chi)=\int_G f(x)\overline{\chi(x)}\,dx.
\]
Changing the exponential convention changes signs or factors of \(2\pi\), but not the dual group itself.

**Warning.** Here “character” means a continuous homomorphism into \(\mathbb T\). It is not the same notion as the [[algebra-representation-theory/character|character of a finite-dimensional representation]], and \(\widehat G\) is not the [[harmonic-analysis/unitary-dual|unitary dual]] used for a general nonabelian group.
